And that brings us to paleosols, which I currently regard as the strongest of these three tests.

A genuine paleosol is not merely a discoloration or an erosional surface. Well-developed examples can contain soil horizons, root structures, mineral alteration, leaching profiles, carbonate accumulations, clay redistribution, and other evidence indicating modification while exposed to an atmosphere and terrestrial hydrology. USGS work on Carboniferous successions identifies multiple superposed paleosols, including inferred Ultisol-, Inceptisol-, Entisol-, and Histosol-like profiles, and interprets them as successive episodes of sedimentation, exposure, pedogenesis, peat accumulation, and renewed flooding.

The problematic architecture is therefore:

D1​→P1​→D2​→P2​→coal→D3​​

where D is deposition and P is genuine soil development.

If all of that has to occur within a Flood year, the available time becomes restrictive very quickly.

Suppose the catastrophic stratigraphic record contained only 100 genuine terrestrial stabilization intervals. Even with the entire 365-day year allocated to them:

100365​=3.65 days/profile.

With 1,000:

0.365 days/profile.

That plainly cannot produce ordinary mature soils through conventional weathering, biological colonization, eluviation, illuviation, and horizon development.

So one of three things must follow.

Either many structures identified as paleosols are actually **rapidly generated pedogenic-looking alteration surfaces** produced hydrothermally or diagenetically.

Or a substantial fraction of those paleosols belong to the **50 kyr non-catastrophic HN​ interval**, rather than the Flood.

Or the one-year HC​ assignment of the entire Paleozoic-Mesozoic package is wrong.

That is precisely the kind of falsification fork we want.

Coal creates a similar constraint. A catastrophic model can readily produce:

vegetation destruction→transport→concentration→rapid burial.

What becomes much harder is:

soil→standing vegetation→peat→burial→new soil→new vegetation

repeated many times within the proposed one-year interval.

That suggests an important correction to our previous stratigraphic mapping.

We should **not yet assume that almost the entire Phanerozoic equals HC​**.

That is actually compatible with your stated chronology because you have:

available before the Flood.

That is enough time for real soils, forests, peat systems, reefs, river channels, lakes, sedimentary basins, and repeated non-catastrophic hydrotectonic changes.
