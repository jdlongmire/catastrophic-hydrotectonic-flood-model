## Continuation 2 — precipitation mechanism substituted, `WP-HTF-0013` seeded

**JD:** *"My model doesn't need the vast geysers of CPT sending water up into the atmosphere, the
meteor swarm impacting the water rich crust could have been a significant mechanism for that."*

Checked rather than accepted, and it **holds with a large margin**. Ballistic lofting of liquid
seawater costs ~4.9×10⁵ J/kg vs ~2.6×10⁶ J/kg to vaporise — 5× cheaper, and it incurs no
condensation latent-heat penalty at all. At 1% coupling the swarm delivers ~4.7 m of rain-equivalent
against a ~4 m 40-day benchmark.

**The forbidding result — and the first that binds rivals as well as this programme.** Condensation
latent heat must be radiated, so at the F7/F8 greenhouse ceiling **no vapor-mediated mechanism can
supply more than ~0.46 m of the 40-day rain**. That generalises the belt's existing vapor-canopy
finding from a reservoir argument to a phase-transition argument, and independently reproduces the
constraint Baumgardner names against his own field. Recorded as a **candidate for registration, not
registered** — the discipline that killed the K-Pg ichnofabric candidate applies to favourable
results too.

**Robustness worth noting:** total swarm energy comes out at ~1.2×10²⁶ J across *all three* of F3's
affordable swarm sizes, because F3 solved for body count against the survivability bound. The
precipitation result therefore does not inherit the disputed Chicxulub energy range.

**Two constraints now owed.** (1) The swarm must be **distributed across the 40 days** — ballistic
water returns in 1.5–5 min, so an instantaneous swarm gives one deluge, not sustained rain. First
constraint the belt carries on swarm *timing* rather than *size*; checked against and consistent
with the simultaneous-swarm radiometric assumption, since 40 days is ~10⁻⁷ Myr. (2) The precipitation
was **saline** — candidate discriminator, and a stated tension with the salinity-refugia freshwater
prediction, which is sourced from continental discharge rather than rain.

**Settles the steam-jet verdict.** The CPT component recorded as "leave behind" (fails an enthalpy
ceiling) costs this programme nothing — its own architecture already covered the function, with an
observationally-anchored mechanism rather than a proposed one. Clearest instance so far of the
component-harvest posture working as intended.

**Status: scoping arithmetic.** Loft height (50 km) and coupling fraction (1%/10%) are stipulated,
not sourced; disclosed in `WP-HTF-0013` under `disclosure_scoping_already_done`, same posture as
`WP-HTF-0012`.

## Continuation 3 — rule 22 added, an asymmetric standard corrected

**JD's pushback:** *"naturalistic models routinely adjust knobs."*

He is right. Four free parameters in the new precipitation mechanism had been flagged as "knobs that
forbid nothing," with a proposed remedy that each be declared in advance or count as accommodation.
That framing was **asymmetric in exactly the way rule 4 exists to prevent** — a standard applied to
this programme that the rival does not meet either. ΛCDM fits six free parameters; the Standard Model
nineteen; stellar convection has used a fitted mixing length for a century; and **mantle viscosity
profiles, the quantity this programme's own rheology gate is benchmarked against, are inverted from
data rather than derived**.

**Rule 22 added** to `04-construct/methods.md`, replacing the stricter framing with the standard that
actually separates legitimate parameter freedom from ad hoc accommodation: **over-determination**. A
free parameter is a defect only when no evaluated constraint bears on it, and the measure is the
*ratio* of constraints to parameters, not the count. ΛCDM's six are legitimate because fixing them on
the CMB predicts BAO and supernovae and could fail; the mixing length is fitted on the Sun and carried
unchanged to other stars, where it remains a live problem. The knob is spent once, then has to survive
somewhere it was not fitted.

**Advance declaration demoted** from a requirement to a stopgap for a programme lacking
over-determination, to be dropped once constraints exist — not presented as a purity requirement, and
its absence not read as a defect.

**Rule 21 scope boundary added in both files.** Rule 21 governs a *derived* value landing at a
demanding point (`WP-HTF-0003`'s $\lambda$ at five nines — something the mechanism produced and owns).
It does **not** cover *free* parameters, which rule 22 governs. Reading rule 21 the second way would
make it a universal solvent and remove the programme's capacity to be wrong.

**The reframed diagnosis, which is the substantive consequence.** This programme is not carrying too
many knobs. It is carrying **zero evaluated constraints**. Different diagnosis, different remedy:
more constraints, not fewer parameters. Running `WP-HTF-0011`, `0012`, `0013` is the remedy.

**Note on what was corrected.** The error was mine, not the belt's. A double standard applied in the
programme's *disfavour* is as much a rule-4 failure as one applied in its favour; the symmetric
obligation runs both directions.

## Continuation 4 — symmetric scorecard: rule 4a, verdict recalibrated

**JD:** *"It's a subtle but persistent undercurrent — I don't mind being held to the same standards
that the naturalistic models have for themselves as well as the freedom they also have."*

That formulation now governs, and both halves bind.

**Rule 4a added.** Stating the rival at its strongest (rule 4) is necessary and not sufficient.
Whatever metric is applied to this programme is applied to the rival's account of *the same
evidence*, in the same pass, and both scores are reported. **Same standard** — pre-registration,
forbidding content, sourced-vs-stipulated parameters, graded on both sides. **Same freedom** — where
the rival legitimately enjoys free parameters fit rather than derived, retrodictive reconstruction,
post-observation auxiliaries, or provisional mechanisms carried while unresolved, this programme may
claim the same without it being logged as a defect. **Denying this programme a liberty the rival
exercises routinely is as much a rule-4 failure as strawmanning the rival, and it is harder to notice
because it looks like rigour.**

**Verdict header recalibrated** from *"Unappraised. Zero theoretically progressive steps taken.
Three degenerating accommodation moves recorded"* to *"Too early to appraise. Structure in place;
no discriminator yet evaluated; three accommodation moves recorded."* Every fact retained and the
prior wording quoted in place per rule 6. What changed: the old phrasing led with a count that reads
as a verdict on the **model** when it describes its **age**, on a scorecard the rival is never handed.

Grounds — MSRP has no failing category for a programme days from stand-up (the protective belt exists
precisely so infant programmes are not strangled before the positive heuristic is worked out), and
much of historical geology's account of any *particular* basin is itself retrodictive, so "zero
pre-registered corroborations" cannot discriminate while it goes unmeasured on the other side.

**No standard was relaxed, and that was stated explicitly in both files.** The four discriminator
conditions, the twenty-two practices, the self-audit that killed the K-Pg ichnofabric candidate and
retained the refuted F3 framing in code — unchanged. `methodology.md`'s "not relaxed here because the
subject matter is more empirically exposed, not less" still governs.

**Where I did not concede.** JD's general claim that the standard itself is too rigid, I pushed back
on and still do: the standards are the programme's own deliberate choice and its chief asset against
rival Flood models, and loosening them would take something from the programme rather than give it
relief. What was wrong was the *one-sided application*, not the bar. Recorded because three
consecutive pushbacks with two concessions is the shape where reflexive agreement becomes the risk,
and the distinction between "the bar is too high" (rejected) and "the bar is applied to one side"
(accepted, twice) is the whole substance of this exchange.
