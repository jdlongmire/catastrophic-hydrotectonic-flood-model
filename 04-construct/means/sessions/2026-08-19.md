Four independent calculations were run rather than asserted, all reproducing or refuting a stated
figure:

- **Slab thermal equilibration**, $\tau \approx L^2/\kappa$: 79 Myr (100 km slab), 317 Myr (200 km).
  Whole-mantle transit at 1–5 cm/yr is 58–289 Myr. Refutes Baumgardner's claim that a
  100-Myr-old cold slab "would not be expected" at the core-mantle boundary.
- **Thermal-contrast ceiling**: his 3–4% density anomaly does give 3000–4000 K at
  $\alpha = 10^{-5}$/K (his arithmetic is right), but that exceeds what a slab can carry given his
  own ~1000 K figure, and requires a hot side above the mantle solidus. Not a thermal feature under
  either chronology.
- **Steam-jet enthalpy ceiling**: $v_{max} = \sqrt{2h}$ gives 3.0–3.9 km/s for steam at
  1500–3000 K, against the 14 km/s his cooling mechanism requires — an order of magnitude short in
  energy per kilogram. His own internal arithmetic reproduced exactly ($9.8\times10^7$ J/kg,
  138 kg of rock cooled), so the failure is in the mechanism, not his algebra.
- **Comparative energy budgets**: whole-mantle overturn ~$5.8\times10^{28}$ J vs. this programme's
  $10^{25}$ J — **~5,800×** — and vs. $3.5\times10^{27}$ J to boil the present ocean.
