# The Hard Core

> **Tier:** 1-hypothesis. **Lakatos role:** the **hard core** of the research programme — the commitments held immune from refutation by the *negative heuristic*. Refutation is not aimed here; it is directed at the [protective belt](../2-theory/). This file enumerates *what is in the core*, so the negative heuristic can be applied without ambiguity.
>
> See [`../0-program-methods/METHODOLOGY.md`](../0-program-methods/METHODOLOGY.md) for the apparatus. This programme has no position paper yet. The initial research is [`../research/GPT-hydrotectonic-flood-model.md`](../research/GPT-hydrotectonic-flood-model.md); the core stated below is JD's own framing of it (2026-08-16), not something derived from that transcript.

## The immune commitments

1. **Genesis records real historical events** — creation, the Fall, the Flood. Textual-historical realism, held by decision, on the same footing as the equivalent commitment in [CAC](https://github.com/jdlongmire/creation-actualization-cosmology)'s hard core.

2. **No deep-time entailment.** What is denied is the *entailment* from the observational corpus to a multi-Gyr history. What is not asserted is that the corpus is inconsistent with one. The modal strength is deliberate — inherited directly from CAC's CRE-008 — and the programme holds it there.

3. **A historical global Flood occurred.** A single, real, geographically global catastrophic event — not a regional flood generalized in the telling, not a mythologized memory of something smaller.

4. **A real, non-zero creation-to-Fall interval exists.** Genesis does not fix its length. The core commits only to its existence, not its duration.

5. **Post-catastrophe natural law is stable.** Ordinary physical law governs interpretation of the geological and hydrological record from the close of the Flood-year catastrophe forward. Intervention is the exception; law is the rule. This is what makes the belt testable at all.

## The warrant for immunity — by *decision*, stated openly

This programme does not claim the commitments above are logically necessary, or that a reader is irrational to reject them. The core rests on the historical reliability of Genesis and on a reading of the creation-to-Fall interval as real but unspecified in length. That is orthodox Lakatos: the core is immune **by methodological decision**, adopted so the programme has something stable to develop.

Two consequences follow:

- **The core is not a scientific claim and is not offered as one.** What must earn scientific standing is the [belt](../2-theory/) and, above all, the [predictions](../3-prediction/).
- **Rejecting the core is a rational option** for a reader who does not share the commitment. The programme's claim on such a reader's attention is not the core but whether the belt generates content that forbids something.

A narrower disclosure specific to this programme: commitment 4 (a real creation-to-Fall gap) is a distinctive reading. Most treatments that address the question at all place the Fall shortly after creation; Genesis does not fix the interval either way. Holding it open, rather than assuming brevity, is this programme's choice — stated plainly here rather than left implicit in a chronology number that shows up later without explanation.

## What is *not* in the core

Everything adjustable — the whole of the protective belt.

| The specific Flood duration (~1 yr) | [`2-theory/kinematics/`](../2-theory/kinematics/) | A reading of the Genesis narrative's internal day-counts. Textually argued but not core — the same way CAC treats its Day Four reading as belt despite strong textual grounding |

Aiming *modus tollens* at the belt rather than the core is the negative heuristic in practice. Aiming it at the core is a category error about what kind of commitment the core is — but so is treating a threatened belt hypothesis (most likely candidate: the stratigraphic mapping, given how often it has already moved) as though it inherited the core's immunity. The [appraisal log](../3-prediction/appraisal.md) exists to catch the second failure, since it is the one this programme is more likely to commit.
