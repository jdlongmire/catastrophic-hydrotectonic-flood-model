# Rheology / Heat Budget

Belt content — and the programme's critical-path gate. See [`../../03-prediction/discriminators.md`](../../03-prediction/discriminators.md).

**From the initial research:** required transient viscosity during the catastrophic interval is 10¹³–10¹⁴ Pa·s (vs. present upper-mantle viscosity 10²⁰–10²¹ Pa·s), derived by requiring deformation stress stay near rock-strength limits (~10⁸ Pa) at the kinematically-required velocities. A qualitative feedback mechanism is proposed (hydration → weakening → velocity increase → deformation heating → further weakening), consistent with Baumgardner-style runaway-subduction proposals in the creationist literature and with mainstream findings that olivine rheology is strongly water- and temperature-dependent. The magnitude of the required drop (6–8 orders of magnitude from present values) is stated; whether the feedback can plausibly reach it is not shown.

**Independently checked (2026-08-16):** the model's own mechanism is water content in transition-zone minerals (ringwoodite/wadsleyite), not partial melt — so the requirement should be checked against water-specific weakening, not a partial-melt floor. It was initially benchmarked against the wrong regime (partial-melt subduction-channel weak zones, ~10¹⁹ Pa·s), giving a 5–6 order-of-magnitude gap. Checked against the correct mechanism instead: Fei et al. 2017 (*Science Advances*, "A nearly water-saturated mantle transition zone inferred from mineral viscosity") measured dislocation mobility (inversely related to viscosity) in ringwoodite specifically, finding ~3.5 orders of magnitude reduction from anhydrous to fully water-saturated — the largest documented water-specific effect on the correct mineral. Starting from a standard transition-zone baseline (~10²¹ Pa·s) and stacking that effect reaches ~10¹⁷–10¹⁸ Pa·s, still short of the required 10¹³–10¹⁴ Pa·s by **~3–4 orders of magnitude** — a real, correctly-targeted mechanism that narrows but does not close the gap. See [`../../dev-notes/20260816-water-weakening-mechanism.md`](../../dev-notes/20260816-water-weakening-mechanism.md) for sourcing — confidence upgraded to HIGH (2026-08-16) after obtaining Fei et al.'s primary full text via PMC5462500; the dev-note also carries the authors' own stated caveats (postglacial-rebound-region inference, temperature dependence, unresolved conductivity discrepancies).

## Rescoped, 2026-08-16 — localized to subduction channels, not the bulk transition zone

This gate no longer gates *everything* downstream. JD's prior hydroplaning slice ([`kinematics/README.md`](../kinematics/README.md)) supplies a separate, quantified mechanism (Terzaghi effective-stress collapse, ~100× heat reduction) for bulk lateral translation that does not depend on mantle viscosity at all. This gate is now scoped narrowly to the vertical question only: can slabs descend into the transition zone within the Flood year, in the smaller volume actually occupied by active subduction channels — not the whole transition zone.

That rescoping is a genuine narrowing, not a rhetorical one: it's the same move mainstream geodynamics already makes with "subduction-channel weak zones" (the very comparison this gate was originally, wrongly, benchmarked against — see the entry above). A localized volume needing 3–4 orders of magnitude of softening, where water content is plausibly most concentrated anyway (the down-going slab), is a materially easier case than the same requirement applied globally. It is **not yet a closed case** — no calculation has been done showing the localized-channel version is achievable, only that the ask is smaller than previously framed.

**Open, added to ROADMAP item 1:** a vertical-descent rate calculation for subduction-channel material specifically has not been done — the existing kinematics work (`../kinematics/README.md`) covers lateral hydroplaning velocity, not vertical slab descent. This is a distinct calculation from both the lateral-velocity reconciliation and the viscosity-magnitude question, and none of the three has been completed yet.

**Open (ROADMAP item 1, gating the vertical-subduction / deep-water-transport half of the model):** quantify total tectonic heat generated at the required velocities against total plausible heat-removal capacity (evaporation, convection, precipitation, radiation, hypercane-scale circulation), following the initial research's own proposed test — "give the hypercane hypothesis its best possible quantitative case." This is the programme's first computational work; scripts land in [`code/`](code/) under the discipline in [`../../00-program-methods/research-practices.md`](../../00-program-methods/research-practices.md) §Computational work — sourced parameters, deterministic and re-runnable from the script's own header, no undisclosed fitting, refuted attempts kept and labeled rather than deleted.

## Energy-partition completeness check, 2026-08-17 — the programme's first computational work

Code: [`code/energy_partition_bounds.py`](code/energy_partition_bounds.py). Deterministic, stdlib-only, no fitted parameters. Run with `python3 02-theory/rheology/code/energy_partition_bounds.py`. Falsification conditions F1–F3 were fixed in the script header before the run, per [`research-practices.md`](../../00-program-methods/research-practices.md) rule 1.

**What prompted it.** An external objection (2026-08-17): *"You need energy to start the move, and an equal and opposite amount of energy to stop it. Friction is the loss in the middle of this. Reduce friction and you still have to deal with the car crash."* The objection located a real gap in the documentation rather than in the physics: the published partition in [`POSITION-PAPER.md`](../../00-program-methods/POSITION-PAPER.md) §3.2 (94.2% residual PE / 5.0% friction / 0.5% viscous / 0.25% seismic) partitions the gravitational potential energy release only, and the repository contained no kinetic-energy accounting anywhere. The impact-trigger energy of Figure 1 was likewise unquantified.

**F1 — bulk kinetic energy (the "stopping energy"). PASS.** At this programme's own stated velocity (9–40 cm/s), bulk KE of the moving continental mass is 0.011% to 0.416% of the friction allocation, against a declared threshold of 1%. Omitting the term from §3.2 is harmless. Deposited entirely in the present ocean it would produce ~10⁻⁵ to ~4×10⁻⁴ K. The objection does not land: the stopping term sits roughly three orders of magnitude below the friction term it was claimed to dominate, because kinetic energy scales as $v^2$ and this mechanism is slow-over-vast-distance, the regime where path-integrated dissipation dominates endpoint kinetic energy.

**F2 — velocity ceiling. New internal constraint.** Requiring bulk KE not to exceed the budget yields a hard ceiling of **27.7 to 38.6 m/s** (range reflecting the continental-mass dispute below), and a far tighter practical ceiling of **6.2 to 8.6 m/s** if KE is held within the friction allocation. This independently corroborates the rejection of the source document's disputed 30 m/s figure ([`../kinematics/README.md`](../kinematics/README.md), §velocity reconciliation): at 30 m/s bulk KE is 60% to 117% of the *entire* 10²⁵ J budget, leaving nothing for the 94.2% residual-PE term the partition requires. The 30 m/s figure was already rejected on a unit-conversion argument; it is now also refuted on energy conservation, by a separate route. **Forbids:** any future kinematic revision proposing continental velocity above this ceiling.

**F3 — impact-trigger energy. Open tension, and the harder finding.** Holding total impact heat within the model's own total dissipation allocation (5.75% of 10²⁵ J = 5.75×10²³ J) affords **0.01 to 1.38 Chicxulub-equivalent bodies**. N_max exceeds one only under the cheapest cited Chicxulub estimate (~4.18×10²³ J); across the comprehensive assessment range (1.3×10²⁴ to 5.8×10²⁵ J) the budget affords less than one body, and at the high end a single impactor would deposit ~9.9 K of ocean-equivalent heat on its own. The "swarm of large meteor impacts" framing used in the [README](../../README.md) and the video summaries is **not obviously compatible** with the thermal allocation as published, and the tension sharpens as the impactor estimate rises.

Two honest qualifications on F3, both carried in the script header. The calculation assumes *all* deposited impact energy becomes terrestrial heat, which is the severest form of the constraint rather than a best estimate; ejecta and radiative loss to space are not modeled and would relax it by an unquantified factor. And the founding research already anticipates the direction of the answer, arguing that impacts should not be "the engines that literally shove the continents across the planet" and that negative slab buoyancy plus gravitational disequilibrium supply "much of the sustained mechanical energy," which it says "greatly reduces the impact-energy requirement" ([`../../research/GPT-hydrotectonic-flood-model.md`](../../research/GPT-hydrotectonic-flood-model.md) lines 199–217). That reduces the energy the trigger must *supply*; it does not bound the energy an impactor of a given size *deposits*. Resolving F3 needs either an explicit impactor-size budget or a modeled partition of impact energy between heat, ejecta, and radiation.

**Disputed inputs, reported rather than smoothed** ([`research-practices.md`](../../00-program-methods/research-practices.md) rule 3). Continental crust mass is carried as a range, 1.34×10²² to 2.60×10²² kg, because published estimates disagree by roughly a factor of two depending on whether submerged margins are included and which density model is used (2600 to 2835 kg/m³); the total-crust figure including oceanic crust is ~2.77×10²² kg. Sources: CRUST2.0-based mass/composition estimates (Peters & Sobolev, AGU 2007 V33A-1161) resting on Christensen & Mooney (1995), *JGR Solid Earth* 100, 9761–9788. Chicxulub impactor energy is likewise carried as a range, 1.3×10²⁴ to 5.8×10²⁵ J from the multi-model assessment in Durand-Manterola & Cordero-Tercero (2014), arXiv:1403.6391, against commonly cited single figures near 10²³ and 4.18×10²³ J. Both at confidence **MEDIUM** (specific figures from secondary summaries; neither primary read in full).

**What this does not resolve.** Heat *removal* capacity is untouched, so ROADMAP item 1 proper remains open, and this script bounds generation terms only. The viscosity gate above is unaffected. The 10²⁵ J budget and its four-way split are imported from Longmire (2025) v2.5 and taken as given here: this checks the partition for **completeness, not correctness**, and every ratio above moves if the underlying budget does. Vertical/subduction energetics are not addressed.
