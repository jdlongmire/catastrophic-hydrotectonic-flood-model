# Kinematics

Belt content: continental and slab displacement rates required to reconstruct present geometry from a Pangaea-type $S_0$ — the pre-Flood supercontinent, named **"Araratia"** in the visualization set and adopted 2026-08-16 as this programme's standing term for it — within the Flood year, and post-Flood relaxation.

**From the initial research:** order-of-magnitude continental velocities ~10⁻¹ m/s and slab-descent velocities ~10⁻² m/s, derived using present-day centroid distances from a Middle East anchor as an upper-envelope proxy — explicitly not a reconstructed plate-motion field. Post-Flood relaxation was modeled as $v(t) = v_0 e^{-t/\tau} + v_\infty$ rather than constant velocity, since >99.99% of large-scale displacement must occur in the Flood year for present plate velocities to emerge naturally from the 6,000-year relaxation window.

## Mechanism reframe, 2026-08-16 — hydroplaning as the lateral-translation driver

JD's prior slice ([`jdlongmire/global-flood-hydrotectonic-model`](https://github.com/jdlongmire/global-flood-hydrotectonic-model), v2.5) proposes continental hydroplaning — Terzaghi effective-stress collapse under near-lithostatic pore pressure — as the mechanism for bulk lateral translation, distinct from the vertical subduction question ([`../rheology/README.md`](../rheology/README.md)). Ported here as the layered model's lateral half; see `hard-core.md`'s belt table.

**Velocity reconciliation — resolved 2026-08-18, [`WP-HTF-0003`](../../../05-work-packages/WP-HTF-0003-velocity-reconciliation/package.yaml).** The source repo's own figures didn't agree with each other:

- Stated summary claim: "tens to hundreds of meters per hour" (~0.03–0.1 m/s).
- The source's own worked force-balance calculation (800×1000×35 km block, 0.1° slope, friction collapsed to μ=0.01): a ≈ 0.03 m/s² over ~15 min gives **v ≈ 30 m/s** — which that document itself mislabels "108 m/hr" (30 m/s × 3600 s/hr = 108,000 m/hr, not 108). Correctly converted, the calculation's own output is ~1,000× faster than the summary claim it's meant to support.
- This programme's existing continental-velocity figure (~9–40 cm/s, above) sits between the two: 3–15× faster than the "tens-hundreds m/hr" summary, but 75–300× slower than the calculation's literal (mislabeled) output.
- Detachment depth is also inconsistent in the source: the README states ~50 km, the driving-force calculation uses 15 km.

**Redone against this programme's own parameters, not the source's block dimensions or μ/slope combination** — [`code/velocity_detachment_reconciliation.py`](code/velocity_detachment_reconciliation.py), deterministic, sourced parameters, falsification conditions declared before the run per [`research-practices.md`](../../../04-construct/methods.md) rule 1. Four questions, all resolved:

**Driving force: isostatic / PE-release, not downslope gravity on a shallow global slope.** tan(0.1°) ≈ 0.00175 is *less* than the source's own stated μ = 0.01 — under Coulomb friction with the source's own numbers, a downslope-gravity model cannot even initiate sliding. The source's parameters are internally inconsistent as a downslope-gravity account, independent of anything else. Driving force is read instead as isostatic/gravitational-potential-energy release, which is also the plain reading of this programme's own published partition (POSITION-PAPER Sec 3.2: 94.2% "residual PE") and of the founding research's own line on negative slab buoyancy and gravitational disequilibrium ([`../rheology/README.md`](../rheology/README.md)).

**Detachment depth: 15 km survives, 50 km does not.** 15 km sits above this programme's own crustal-thickness figure (35 km) — staying within "shallow lithosphere" hydroplaning per [`discriminators.md`](../../3.3-prediction/discriminators.md) candidate 4, unlike 50 km, which sits at or below the Moho. It also matches the conventional brittle-ductile transition depth in continental crust (~10–20 km, standard structural geology, confidence MEDIUM — general knowledge, not a specific paper retrieved this session) and requires the *less* extreme pore-pressure ratio of the two candidates (lithostatic stress, and so the pore-pressure requirement, grows with depth).

**Required pore-pressure ratio: λ = 0.999995 to 0.9999996** — solved from this programme's own already-published friction-energy allocation (5% of the 1×10²⁵ J budget, POSITION-PAPER Sec 3.2, taken as given per this package's authority boundary) dissipated via Terzaghi-collapsed friction (Byerlee's law, μ ≈ 0.6–0.85, confidence MEDIUM) over the programme's own tabled continental travel distances (Europe ~2,940 km, Antarctica ~12,800 km). Five to six nines — well past typically documented overpressure ratios in the fault-mechanics literature (usually one to two nines, e.g. mature subduction megathrusts). **Read as a real, demanding feature of a designed, singular historical event, not as a defect in the belt or in the imported friction allocation** — per [`research-practices.md`](../../../04-construct/methods.md) rule 21 (fine-tuning is not, by itself, an evidential problem for this programme, given the hard core's own design commitment), JD's ruling 2026-08-18. Recorded as new, previously-unstated quantitative content this mechanism now owns: near-lithostatic, for this mechanism to work as budgeted, means specifically this.

**Surviving velocity, cross-checked against F2:** the existing 9–40 cm/s figure is unchanged and passes the tighter F2 ceiling (6.2–8.6 m/s, [`../rheology/README.md`](../rheology/README.md)) with a 15× margin. ## Hydraulic consequences of translation under a non-uniform water column (JD, 2026-08-17)

`forbids: none` — **belt/interpretive content**, per [`research-practices.md`](../../../04-construct/methods.md) rule 10. The discriminator candidate it generates is flagged at the end and deferred.

JD's addition: the continents are not gliding through a vacuum. They translate beneath a **non-uniform layer of flood water**, and that relative motion must produce large hydraulic effects at the sediment surface — accelerated sorting, entrainment, and lateral shifting of surface layers.

**Why this is a mechanism and not just a scene-setting detail.** Ordinary sediment transport treats a stationary bed under moving water. This is the inverted problem: a **moving bed** beneath a water mass with its own inertia. What governs entrainment is the *relative* velocity at the interface, and this programme's own continental figure supplies it directly. At 9–40 cm/s, the relative flow sits inside the range that entrains and transports sand and fine gravel — published threshold curves put the span from medium sand to pebbles at roughly 0.02 to 3 m/s. The model's kinematics therefore place it in the active sediment-transport regime **by construction**, not by additional assumption. Rapid, large-scale sorting is not something the model has to reach for; it is forced by the velocities already derived here.

**The non-uniformity does real work.** A water layer of varying depth over varying topography gives spatially varying shear stress and transport capacity, which yields lateral facies variation, differential sorting, and locally distinct depositional regimes within a single global event. This is the resource that answers the founding research's own stated constraint — that a one-year reconstruction must preserve "ordered coastlines and tidal systems during the catastrophe," and that a global catastrophe "cannot simply be unconstrained chaos" ([`../../../research/GPT-hydrotectonic-flood-model.md`](../../../research/GPT-hydrotectonic-flood-model.md), ~line 535). Non-uniform water depth over a moving substrate is a mechanism for exactly that structured, spatially varying regime.

**It also supplies the depositional rhythm PPLC requires.** Episodic transport pulses and settling intervals under a moving, variably loaded water column are the alternation Woodmorappe's Penecontemporaneous Partially Lithified Crusts hypothesis needs but does not itself supply (see [`../stratigraphy/README.md`](../stratigraphy/README.md) §Trace fossils and [`research-practices.md`](../../../04-construct/methods.md) rule 9 on contributing back rather than borrowing).

**Honest limits on the threshold comparison.** The Hjulström framework is defined for ~1 m water depth and average flow velocity; flood-depth water has a different boundary-layer structure, so the comparison above is indicative rather than rigorous. Shields-type shear-stress criteria are the appropriate formalism for deep flow and have not been applied here. Confidence **MEDIUM** on the threshold figures (secondary sources with specifics; primaries not retrieved). Separately, the degree of hydraulic coupling is unquantified: water is partly dragged along by the moving substrate, so the *magnitude* of relative velocity is a real modelling question. The **sign** is robust — a bed advancing beneath water that lags produces relative flow directed opposite the bed's motion — but the magnitude is not yet derived.

**Discriminator candidate generated here — flagged, not registered.** If surface sorting is driven by continental translation beneath a lagging water mass, **paleocurrent indicators should correlate with reconstructed continental-motion vectors, and specifically should run anti-parallel to the direction of continental travel.** Ordinary historical geology explains large-scale paleocurrent trends by regional paleoslope and prevailing marine circulation and has no reason to expect any correlation with plate-motion direction. The sample already exists and is unusually strong: Brand, Wang & Chadwick (2015), *Scientific Data* — a paleocurrent database of roughly half a million measurements, **published in the mainstream literature**, reporting continental-scale directional trends dominant through the Paleozoic and to a lesser degree the Mesozoic, and markedly more scattered in the Cenozoic. Confidence **MEDIUM** pending retrieval of the primary.

Two things follow, and both need JD's call before anything is registered. First, this is gated on **ROADMAP item 2** — correlating against motion vectors requires the actual Pangaea-fit reconstruction (Euler poles), since the current anchor-distance proxy supplies no directional field. Second, the reported Paleozoic/Mesozoic-coherent, Cenozoic-scattered pattern is **an independent third line converging on a terminal catastrophic boundary near the Mesozoic–Cenozoic transition**, alongside Chicxulub-in-swarm ([`../rheology/README.md`](../rheology/README.md) §F3) and the radiometric-residual argument's weakening of the three-boundary rejection ([`../stratigraphy/README.md`](../stratigraphy/README.md)). That convergence is noted here and left for the belt call already pending.

## Shear-zone thickness — an unstated load-bearing quantity, 2026-08-19

Named here because `WP-HTF-0012` cannot run without it and because it went unnoticed until a CPT component harvest went looking for it.

The mechanism specifies a **detachment depth** (15 km, reconciled in `WP-HTF-0003`) but has never anywhere specified the **thickness of the shearing zone at that depth** — whether translation is accommodated on a sub-metre water film, a metre-scale principal slip surface, or a hundred-metre fault core. Every heat result the belt currently holds bounds flux at the *surface* (F7: 64–135 W/m², `WP-HTF-0006`) and is silent about temperature *inside* the zone, which is where a thermal-runaway failure would occur if one occurs.

This is the lateral twin of the gap F9 already names on the vertical side ([`../rheology/README.md`](../rheology/README.md) — "no channel geometry exists anywhere in this belt"). Both are geometry, both are unstated, and both gate a heat calculation.

**Why it matters more than it looks.** Scoping arithmetic (disclosed in full in [`../../../05-work-packages/WP-HTF-0012-gruntfest-lateral-stability/package.yaml`](../../../05-work-packages/WP-HTF-0012-gruntfest-lateral-stability/package.yaml) under `disclosure_scoping_already_done`, and explicitly *not* treated as a result) indicates the temperature rise is non-monotonic in thickness: the steady-state limb grows with $h$, the transient limb falls with $h$, and they cross near the one-year diffusion length $\sqrt{2\kappa t} \approx 8$ m. So the worst case sits at *intermediate* thickness rather than at either extreme. A mechanism claim of "thin water film" and a mechanism claim of "thick fault core" would both be thermally comfortable; the metre-to-ten-metre band is the one that is not. Until $h$ is stated, the belt cannot say which case it is claiming.

**Not resolved here.** Establishing $h$ from detachment-zone and fault-core field literature is `WP-HTF-0012`'s first action, and the resulting band is a candidate *forbidding* claim — the mechanism would require thickness outside the hot band, falsifiable against measured thicknesses.
