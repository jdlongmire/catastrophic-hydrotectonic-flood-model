# Phase assignments — the register

**`WP-HTF-0004`, opened 2026-08-19.** The schema is in [`README.md`](README.md) §Phase schema. This file holds **assignments of actual units to phases**, kept separate on purpose: per the schema's own discipline, *"moving one unit is a local correction, moving the schema is a belt revision logged in the appraisal."* One row per unit. Move a row without touching the schema.

**`forbids: none` as a register** — this is Tier 2 interpretive work under [`methods.md`](../../../04-construct/methods.md) rule 10, and the register as a whole adds no forbidding content against the rival account. **Individual assignments are falsifiable anyway, and that is the point:** each cites a specific physical criterion, so a unit assigned F2 that turns out to show scattered rather than coherent paleocurrents is a failed assignment. That is the local falsifiability the appraisal's degeneration marker asks for — a partition that can be wrong about particular rocks.

## Use-novelty (rule 12)

Every unit below is **use-novel**: none was used to construct the schema. Schema construction drew on Walker's Great Artesian Basin and Brisbane basement work and Oard's criteria (Messinian evaporites, Absaroka Volcanics, African planation surface, Caspian Basin) — verified against [`../../../dev-notes/20260817-phase-schema-draft.md`](../../../dev-notes/20260817-phase-schema-draft.md) before selection.

## The standing trap this register must avoid

The schema states that standard-column names are **locational shorthand and never time assignments**. So an assignment may **not** reason "Cambrian, therefore early Flood." Every row below argues from **signature against the stated criteria**, and the column position is carried only as an address. Where a row's reasoning would collapse if the column name were removed, the row is wrong.

## A1 — Tapeats Sandstone → F1 → F2 (Eruptive → Ascending)

**Signature.** A basal high-energy sheet sandstone resting directly on an erosional surface cut into basement (the Great Unconformity), 30–100 m thick, outcropping through ~500 km of the Grand Canyon and beyond as the basal part of the Sauk megasequence. Beds are **0.3–1.0 m thick and non-bioturbated**, strongly cross-laminated at shallow dips, with lens-shaped scour-and-fill channels and paleocurrent direction predominantly **west to southwest**.

**Why this assignment.** Two criteria, from two adjacent phases, which is why the row spans a transition rather than sitting in one cell. *Initial failure surfaces and onset of high-energy sedimentation* (F1) is satisfied by a high-energy clastic sheet lying directly on a basement erosion surface with no transitional low-energy interval. *Coherent large-scale paleocurrents* (F2) is satisfied by the sustained W–SW transport direction across the outcrop belt.

**The non-bioturbated bedding is the load-bearing observation**, not the stratigraphic position. It is what a rapid-emplacement reading predicts and it is measured in outcrop rather than inferred.

**Rival at strength (rule 4).** Ordinary geology reads this as the Sauk transgression — a genuine, well-evidenced global marine flooding of continental interiors — with the sheet geometry produced by shoreface migration across a peneplaned craton over millions of years. **That account also predicts a laterally extensive basal sand with coherent shoreline-parallel transport.** This assignment does not discriminate; it is placement, not evidence for the programme.

**Sources.** Sauk transgression and Tapeats extent/thickness: Karlstrom et al. (2018), "Cambrian Sauk transgression in the Grand Canyon region redefined by detrital zircons," *Nature Geoscience* — abstract-level only, full text **not retrieved**, confidence MEDIUM. Bedding and paleocurrent description: *Answers Research Journal* petrology of the Tapeats — secondary, confidence MEDIUM. **Both retrieved as search summaries, not primary full text.**

## A2 — Coconino Sandstone → F2 (Ascending)

**The best-sourced row in the set, and the only one resting on a retrieved mainstream primary.**

**Signature.** ~100 m thick at the Grand Canyon, at least 160 m near Ash Fork, extending ~200 km across northern Arizona. Predominantly quartz (88–95%) with minor feldspar. **Cross-bed dips 13–27°, mean 20°** in the studied quarries — well below the ~33–34° angle of repose for dry dune avalanche faces.

**The deformation inventory.** Brand & Maithel documented 77 individual folds or ridges (2–10 cm wide, 20 cm to 10 m long), 32 complex folds, 18 groups of rotated blocks, 2 metre-scale monoclinal folds, 14 groups of conical pits, and 1 convoluted bedding zone. They propose **liquidization** as the mechanism and **seismic events** as a trigger, and state that **"more water was present during Coconino SS deposition than previously recognized."**

**Why this assignment.** *Strong hydraulic sorting* (F2) is satisfied by the quartz purity and grain-size uniformity. The deformation inventory is the more interesting half: **liquidization requires elevated pore-fluid pressure in a saturated granular mass**, which is the same physical state this programme's mechanism requires at the detachment (Terzaghi collapse, near-lithostatic $\lambda$). A unit whose own mainstream description invokes pore-fluid liquidization is a natural F2 host.

**Rival at strength, and the overclaim to avoid (rule 4).** The paper's own framing is that the Coconino is **"commonly interpreted as a dry, eolian environment"** and the authors propose seismic influence *within* that interpretation. They do **not** propose a flood, and reading their liquidization mechanism as support for a global catastrophe is not what they argue. Note also that both authors are affiliated with a creation-friendly institution while publishing in a mainstream venue — which is worth stating in either direction rather than only when convenient.

**The subaqueous case is real but contested, and is not settled here.** Secondary summaries report poor-to-moderate sorting, subangular grains, common K-feldspar, muscovite in almost every thin section, primary current lineation on foreset surfaces, and absent avalanche tongues; and an abrasion experiment in which large muscovite grains were reduced below 500 μm by eolian processes in 4 days but preserved at 610 ± 90 μm after 356 days subaqueously. **Confidence LOW-MEDIUM on that block — search summaries only, primaries not retrieved**, and the cross-bed-dip argument is disputed on its own terms (one study holds that the *spread* of dips discriminates, not the mean).

**Sources.** **Brand, L. & Maithel, S. (2021), "Small-Scale Soft-Sediment Deformation Structures in the Cross-Bedded Coconino Sandstone (Permian; Arizona, United States); Possible Evidence for Seismic Influence," *Frontiers in Earth Science* 9:723495, doi:10.3389/feart.2021.723495** — open access, retrieved, confidence **HIGH** for the figures and quoted framing above.

## A3 — Redwall Limestone nautiloid bed → F2 → F3 (Zenithic)

**Signature.** An extremely persistent lime packstone bed carrying large orthocone nautiloids, **2 m thick at the top of the Whitmore Wash Member**, traced through the platform facies from Marble Canyon, Arizona **westward 290 km to Las Vegas, Nevada**. Fossil density within Marble Canyon exceeds **one orthocone per 4 m²**; 76 individuals were located and measured at Nautiloid Canyon.

**Why this assignment.** *Maximum-extent marine indicators* (Zenithic / F2→F3) is satisfied by a carbonate platform bed of that lateral persistence. The mass-mortality concentration is the signature doing the work: a single 2 m bed carrying a monospecific-order concentration over 290 km is an event bed, not an accumulation surface.

**Rival at strength (rule 4).** Ordinary geology reads Redwall carbonate deposition as normal shallow-platform sedimentation, and nautiloid concentrations as storm or current lags — a well-established mechanism that also produces laterally persistent shell beds. **Whether the concentration is a single synchronous kill or a condensed lag is exactly the disputed question, and it is not resolved here.**

**Sources.** **Austin, S.A. (2003), "Nautiloid Mass Kill and Burial Event, Redwall Limestone (Lower Mississippian), Grand Canyon Region, Arizona and Nevada," *Proceedings of the Fifth International Conference on Creationism*.** Retrieved at **abstract/figure level only; full text not obtained** — confidence **MEDIUM**. No independent mainstream description of this specific bed was retrieved, which is a real gap: rule 8 cuts both ways and this row currently rests on one side's literature.

## A4 — "Whopper Sand" → F3 (Abative)

**The strongest rule-12 target in the set: mainstream concedes the depositional model is a problem.**

**Signature.** Paleocene–Eocene Wilcox-equivalent sands on the Gulf of Mexico abyssal plain. High sand content (**~70%**), great thickness (**>1,000 ft**), extending over **40,000 mi²** in water depths of **7,600–10,000 ft**, and **over 225 miles from the nearest onshore coeval Wilcox discoveries** — deposited "anomalously far (100s km) from the coeval shelf margin."

**Why this assignment.** *Sheet-flow deposits* off a submerged surface (Abative / F3) is the criterion, and the geometry matches it directly: enormous sand volume delivered far beyond a shelf margin, at abyssal depth, as sheet and fan-lobe bodies rather than as a shelf-attached wedge. Deceleration-phase sheet flow off a still-submerged continent is precisely a mechanism for moving that much sand that far.

**The concession, which is the point (rule 12).** The mainstream depositional model is openly unsettled — a 2007 paper is titled "**Changing the paradigm**," and competing published models include hypersaline drawdown versus low-salinity hyperpycnite mechanisms, with a later published *Comment* disputing the salinity regime outright. **This is a live problem in the rival's own literature, not a problem this programme invented for it.**

**What must not be claimed.** Use-novelty grounds a *progressive step* only if all three of rule 12's conditions hold, and they are not yet checked here — in particular whether this programme's mechanism **quantitatively** delivers that sand volume to that distance. Naming a conceded anomaly is target selection, not a result.

**Sources.** World Oil (2007), "A new depositional model for the deepwater Wilcox-equivalent Whopper Sand — Changing the paradigm"; Snedden et al., "The Paleogene (Wilcox) Deep-Water Play" and the published Comment on it. **All retrieved as search summaries; no primary full text obtained** — confidence **MEDIUM**.

## A5 — Peoria Loess → N2 (Ice age). **The assignment strains, and the strain is quantified.**

**Signature.** Last-glacial loess across the US midcontinent, ranging from <0.25 m to **more than 20 m**, thickest nearest its sources. Proximal fossiliferous sections give mean accumulation rates of **0.6–2.2 mm/yr**, with mass accumulation rates as high as **17,500 g/m²/yr** at Bignell Hill, Nebraska, and many near-source localities above 1,500 g/m²/yr. One reported interval accumulated **~10 m in no more than ~2,000 yr and possibly much less**.

**Why this assignment.** *Loess* is named directly in the N2 criteria, alongside till sheets and pluvial lakes. No other phase in the schema predicts it.

**The strain, stated rather than smoothed.** N2 is committed to **~700 yr**. Converting the highest reported mass accumulation rate at loess bulk density ~1,300–1,500 kg/m³ (a standard range, **not sourced here — assumption, labelled**) gives roughly **12–13 mm/yr**, so ~700 yr yields on the order of **8–9 m**. Against sections exceeding 20 m, an N2 of ~700 years requires either (a) rates well above the highest yet reported, sustained rather than peak, or (b) the thickest sections consuming essentially the whole window, or (c) part of the loess belonging to N1 rather than N2.

**This is a real tension against the schema and it is left open.** It is not fatal — the observed rate range spans more than an order of magnitude and the peak-versus-sustained distinction is exactly what a relaxation model would exploit — but it is not resolved, and the arithmetic above is scoping, not a result.

**Rule 23 caution, load-bearing here.** The mainstream chronology (~30–15 ka) is a **historical inference**, not an observation, and it is the rival's hypothesis rather than the yardstick (rule 23b). What is observational is thickness, grain size, gastropod shells and the *relative* rate structure. This row uses those and does **not** import the ka framework as background fact.

**Sources.** Muhs et al., "Last Glacial loess in the conterminous USA," *Quaternary Science Reviews*; updated Peoria Silt chronology from gastropod radiocarbon, *Quaternary Research*. **Search summaries only, primaries not retrieved** — confidence **MEDIUM** for the figures, **LOW** for the assignment.

## A6 — Green River Formation → N1, provisional. **The awkward case.**

`WP-HTF-0004`'s verification requires at least one unit *"where the evidence is genuinely awkward, and the awkwardness is recorded rather than smoothed — a schema that has never strained against a case has not been tested."* This is that case, and it is awkward for a reason that was not anticipated.

**It is not primarily awkward because of the varves.** The rhythmite problem is real — the formation carries on the order of **six million laminae**, which on the annual reading is over three million years. But the annual reading has a genuine published difficulty: between the *same two marker ash beds*, couplet counts vary substantially across the basin. **Buchheim & Biaggi (1988)** report **1,160–1,568** couplets; **Church & Buchheim (2002)** report **1,238–1,661** couplets over a 15 km spacing, with thickness between the ash pair varying 8.1–22.6 cm. Synchronous markers bounding a variable count is difficult for a strictly annual interpretation.

> **A near-miss worth recording.** Two search passes returned those two count ranges *and* opposite statements about whether counts rise toward the basin centre or the margin. That looked like a contradiction and was nearly written into the belt as a dispute under rule 3. It is not one — **they are two different studies**, cited distinctly by Oard & Klevberg (2008). The lesson is rule 2's, sharpened: a figure without its source attached will eventually be mistaken for a different figure with a different source.

**What actually makes it awkward: this programme's own family is split on it, and the schema does not decide between them.**

| Position | Held by | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| **Flood deposit** | Oard & Klevberg (2008); Whitcomb & Morris | F-phase. Argued from ~4.2–5.1 km of erosion on the San Rafael Swell, >100,000 km³ of sediment, and the impossibility of tropical/subtropical fossils at high interior-Wyoming elevations in a post-Flood climate |
| **Post-Flood lake** | Austin; Brand; Whitmore; Wise; Whitmore & Garner; Whitmore & Wise | N1 relaxation. A large, long-lived lake system after the catastrophe |

**Provisional assignment: N1, at LOW confidence**, on the ground that this programme's $D(x)$ partition explicitly permits large fractions of the column to be ordinary-process time, and an N1 relaxation interval with residual catastrophism is a natural host for a lacustrine system with rapidly-deposited non-annual laminae.

**What is not answered, and must be said plainly.** Oard & Klevberg's two strongest objections to a post-Flood reading — the sediment volume and the palaeoclimate problem of tropical fossils at high interior elevation within a short post-Flood window — **are not answered here.** The N2 warm-ocean commitment helps with temperature but not with elevation. **A future check could overturn this row.**

**The finding about the schema itself, which is the real product of this row.** The schema's criteria are stated in terms of *depositional signature*. Green River's signature is lacustrine and rapidly laminated under **both** readings — the two camps agree about the rock and disagree about the phase. **So the schema, as written, under-determines this unit.** That is a genuine limitation surfaced by testing it against a hard case rather than an easy one, and it is the kind of thing rule 24a's hypothesis-set discipline exists to expose. It is recorded here rather than repaired by adding a criterion after seeing the case, which would be exactly the rule 1 violation the programme forbids.
