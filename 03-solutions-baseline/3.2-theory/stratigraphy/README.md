### The boundary question, reframed — and it dissolves rather than resolves

**The Flood/post-Flood transition is not a horizon. It is a diachronous phase transition,** $t_C(x)$.

The three searches below failed because they asked for the wrong kind of object: *which single global horizon marks the Flood?* The founding research had already proposed a diachronous front, and a diachronous front is a phase concept. **This is recorded as a mis-specified question, not as three defeats.**

That framing is not invented here. The creationist literature already contains a four-way debate — a low Paleozoic boundary; **K-Pg** (Snelling); **late Cenozoic** (Oard, on fourteen criteria including the Messinian evaporites, the Absaroka Volcanics, the African planation surface); and a **variable** boundary evaluated per area. Notably Snelling himself placed Israel at or near top-Oligocene while generally advocating K-Pg. **This programme adopts the variable/diachronous position**, which its own $t_C(x)$ already was. Confidence **MEDIUM** (secondary summaries).

**Consequence to hold consciously:** this declines to adopt K-Pg as a global boundary, so the earlier three-line convergence on K-Pg is now read as evidence about *where the transition falls in those regions*, not as a global horizon. It also means the belt uses Oard's field results (ice age, paleosols) without adopting his late-Cenozoic global boundary — coherent, but deliberate rather than accidental.

**Stratigraphic mapping — unresolved, and less settled than the first pass suggested.** The initial research tested Cambrian onset, then Permian-Triassic, Triassic-Jurassic, and Cretaceous-Paleogene as candidate catastrophic-phase boundaries, and called all of them failed on the same evidence: paleosols, coal, and reefs recurring past every candidate boundary, including K-Pg. That evidence is real, but whether it's correctly read is a genuine, live dispute among geologists working these exact formations — see [`../../../dev-notes/20260816-boundary-search-literature.md`](../../../dev-notes/20260816-boundary-search-literature.md). ## Depositional-regime partition — $D(x)$, not a Flood/no-Flood binary (2026-08-18)

with the ~50–60 ka pre-Flood interval (hard-core commitment 4's belt estimate) supplying ordinary-process time for reef construction, soils, erosion, river systems, ecological succession, fossilization, and sedimentation — including genuine annual varves, see §Rhythmites below — before any catastrophic mechanism is invoked, and ~5–6 ka post-Flood for further ordinary development.

which makes $t_C(x)$ considerably more powerful than the 2026-08-17 phase-schema draft stated — the transition need not correspond to one universal lithostratigraphic boundary *and* large fractions of column thickness on either side of it can be ordinary-process time, not catastrophic-interval time.

## Radiometric interpretation — assumption captured 2026-08-17, not yet a belt commitment

Until now this programme carried **no stated position on radiometric dating at all**, beyond using AMS radiocarbon's practical ceiling to anchor the creation-to-Fall gap above. That was a gap: the chronology partition above is incompatible with the standard model's absolute ages, and nothing in the belt said why.

JD's stated assumption (2026-08-17): the meteor swarm was **simultaneous**, its products therefore carry **similar radiometric residuals**, and those residuals account for the K-Pg boundary and the geologic column under a different chronology than the standard model. The supporting datum is Austin (1996) on the Mount St Helens dacite — rock that solidified in 1986 yielding K-Ar ages of 0.34 ± 0.06 Ma (feldspar-glass) to 2.8 ± 0.6 Ma (pyroxene concentrate), attributed to excess argon occluded in phenocrysts.

**Captured, deliberately not promoted.** Full analysis with the mainstream counter-case stated at strength is in [`../../../dev-notes/20260817-radiometric-residuals-simultaneous-swarm.md`](../../../dev-notes/20260817-radiometric-residuals-simultaneous-swarm.md). Two problems block promotion. First, "similar" residuals explain why one event's products date *coherently* but cannot produce the column's *ordered* spread (Cambrian ~540 Ma to K-Pg ~66 Ma); similarity predicts a flat apparent-age profile, so an ordering mechanism is needed and none is stated. Second, excess argon is K-Ar/Ar-Ar specific and has no obvious purchase on U-Pb concordia, Rb-Sr, or Sm-Nd, while the column's framework rests substantially on agreement *between* systems that fail in unrelated ways.

**What it does change here, immediately.** The three-boundary rejection recorded below was conducted using the standard model's stratigraphic assignments. If apparent ages are residual-derived, "above the boundary" does not mean "after the flood" in the sense that test assumed, and the rejection may be **mis-specified rather than correct**. That weakens the negative case further than the Oard & Klevberg field dispute alone did, and bears specifically on K-Pg — which is also where the Chicxulub-in-swarm commitment points (see [`../rheology/README.md`](../rheology/README.md) §F3 and [`../../../dev-notes/20260817-start-stop-energy-objection.md`](../../../dev-notes/20260817-start-stop-energy-objection.md)).

## Rhythmites in freshwater basins — refugia as depositional settings (JD, 2026-08-17)

`forbids: none` — **belt/interpretive content** per [`research-practices.md`](../../../04-construct/methods.md) rule 10.

JD's extension of the salinity-refugia mechanism ([`../water-ledger/README.md`](../water-ledger/README.md) §Salinity refugia): the same non-uniformity that preserves pre-Flood freshwater reservoirs also offers a reading of **the layers found at the bottom of freshwater and former-freshwater basins** — the rhythmites conventionally read as varves.

**Why this matters more than most belt additions.** Varve counting is one of the two principal independent chronometers standing against this programme's chronology, the other being radiometric dating. The Green River Formation is the canonical case, with laminated couplets counted in the millions.

**The rival at its strongest** (rule 4). The conventional argument is not merely "these look like layers." A varve is *defined* as laminae deposited in still water within one year. The case that GRF-type couplets are annual rests on seasonal proxies cycling with the couplets (pollen, diatom assemblages, isotopic signals), correlation against independent chronometers such as radiocarbon and dated tephra horizons, and cross-correlation of counts between separate basins. Any rapid-deposition reading has to engage that convergence, not just the couplets themselves.

**Resources already in the creationist literature** (searched per rule 8; confidence **MEDIUM**, secondary-level, primaries not retrieved):

- **Berthault's sedimentation experiments** (French Academy of Sciences, 1986 and 1988; English translations subsequently in creation journals). Multiple laminations form *spontaneously* when heterogeneous sediment is deposited in air, running water, or still water, because particles of differing size spontaneously segregate and stratify. Lamination is shown to be a property of the depositional process, not a clock.
- **Mount St Helens, 12 June 1980.** A 25 ft (7.6 m) stratified pyroclastic deposit accumulated in a few hours from collapse of an eruption plume, consisting of thin, usually alternating fine and coarse pumice-ash laminae, locally cross-bedded. A directly observed case of laminated strata forming catastrophically. Note this is the *same site* JD already invokes for excess argon (§Radiometric interpretation above) — one location supplying two independent arguments.
- **Fish preservation.** Articulated, well-preserved fish require rapid burial, since carcasses decay within weeks even in cold, anoxic bottom water. Laminae bracketing an articulated fish therefore cannot each represent a year.

**A live dispute inside the creationist literature, reported rather than smoothed** (rule 3). The Green River Formation is **not** settled among flood geologists. Michael Oard argues for Flood deposition; John Whitmore, who studied the GRF for his PhD and examined all the Green River basins, argues it is a **post-Flood lacustrine** deposit on paleontological, lithofacies, mineralogical and other grounds, and the two have exchanged published responses. This programme should not cite "the creationist position" on the GRF as though it were one position, and JD's proposal lands squarely inside that disagreement rather than outside it.

**What this model contributes — arguably a third position** (rule 9). Oard's Flood reading struggles with the quiet-standing-water character Whitmore's evidence points to; Whitmore's post-Flood lake reading buys that character at a chronological cost. A **preserved, density-stratified freshwater reservoir persisting *during* the Flood** is structurally distinct from both: it supplies a standing, quiet, lacustrine-character water body *within* the catastrophic interval. The pycnocline provides a settling and trapping interface — density interfaces concentrate suspended load in modern stratified basins — so pulsed sediment delivery from the surrounding turbid flood water across that interface gives rhythmic lamination without annual cycling. Whether this genuinely dissolves the Oard–Whitmore dispute or merely adds a third contested option is open, and should not be asserted until someone competent in that literature evaluates it. Flagging it as a possible contribution, not claiming it.

**A caution this programme's own appraisal requires.** With this addition the belt now carries proposed reinterpretations of **both** principal independent chronometers — radiometric ages as residuals (§Radiometric interpretation) and rhythmites as rapid laminae. Each is individually defensible and each is standard in the wider literature. But [`../../3.3-prediction/appraisal.md`](../../3.3-prediction/appraisal.md) lists among its degeneration markers "a moving target that's never pinned down is a programme that can't be wrong by construction," and a programme that accumulates a reinterpretation for every chronometer approaches exactly that shape. **This is not a reason to stop**, and reinterpreting a chronometer is legitimate belt work. It is a reason to prioritise ROADMAP item 3 — fixing and date-stamping a specific chronology partition as a recorded commitment — so the programme has something chronological that *can* be wrong. Recorded here as a live watch item, per the appraisal's own instruction to catch this pattern rather than to be surprised by it later.

**Open.** Whether the pycnocline-trapping mechanism produces couplet *regularity* of the kind observed is unaddressed; spontaneous segregation (Berthault) and pulsed delivery both produce lamination, but the conventional case rests substantially on rhythmic regularity and its correlation with seasonal proxies. That is the specific thing this reading has to account for, and it has not been attempted.

**Three-regime model, made explicit 2026-08-18** ([`../../../dev-notes/20260818-dfm-boundary-conditions-gap-ledger.md`](../../../dev-notes/20260818-dfm-boundary-conditions-gap-ledger.md) §5), applying the $G_{pre}/G_{Flood}/G_{post}$ partition above specifically to laminated sequences rather than leaving the reading above to imply every lamination needs a catastrophic account:

$$L_{pre} = \text{genuine annual varves over tens of thousands of years}$$
$$L_{Flood} = \text{rapid event/subannual rhythmites}$$
$$L_{post} = \text{post-Flood annual/seasonal accumulation}$$

**Why this is worth stating separately rather than leaving implicit:** it removes an unintended burden the reading above risked carrying — that this model needs a rapid-lamination account for laminated sequences generally. It does not. $L_{pre}$ can be conventional annual varves at no cost to the model, since it falls inside the ~50–60 ka pre-Flood window; only $L_{Flood}$ needs the rapid-deposition mechanism (Berthault, Mount St Helens, PPLC) developed above. This is a **narrowing** of the model's own claim, which cuts against rather than adds to the appraisal's moving-target concern (§A caution, above) — fewer chronometers need reinterpreting, not more.

**The discriminator sharpens accordingly:** not "is this sequence annual" in the abstract, but whether a given sequence's independently-demonstrated annual-cycle count (seasonal proxies, cross-basin correlation) exceeds the chronology available to whichever regime — $L_{pre}$, $L_{Flood}$, or $L_{post}$ — this model's own $D(x)$ partition assigns it to. Testable sequence by sequence; a sharpening of the existing discriminator, not a new one.

## Trace fossils and bioturbation — this model's reading

`forbids: none` — **belt/interpretive content, not a prediction.** This section states how the hydrotectonic model reads ichnofossil evidence. Retrodiction is the appropriate mode at this stage of the programme (see [`../../00-program-methods/research-practices.md`](../../../04-construct/methods.md) rules 8–10 and the Lakatosian "treat budding programmes leniently" posture); the discriminator candidate this material generates is noted at the end and deferred to ROADMAP item 4.

**The challenge in its strongest form.** Ichnofossils are ubiquitous through Phanerozoic sedimentary rock. If each burrowed layer and each individual trace requires a period of quiet colonization, one stratigraphic horizon at a time, the cumulative time demanded is incompatible with a Flood-year deposition of the bulk of that rock. At the K-Pg horizon specifically, the boundary layer is cross-cut vertically by *Zoophycos* and *Chondrites* and penetrated laterally by *Chondrites*, with a trace-fossil assemblage running continuously into the uppermost Maastrichtian (Rodríguez-Tovar & Uchman 2008, *Geobios*; Alegret et al. 2015, *Terra Nova*, "How bioturbation obscured the Cretaceous–Palaeogene boundary record"). Confidence **MEDIUM** — search-summary level, primaries not yet retrieved.

**The reading this model adopts**, following Woodmorappe, J. (2006), "Are soft-sediment trace fossils (ichnofossils) a time problem for the Flood?", *Journal of Creation* 20(2):113–122 (primary text retrieved and read; confidence **HIGH** as to what the paper argues, with the underlying claims inheriting whatever confidence his own cited sources carry — not independently audited here):

1. **Identification is less secure than it appears.** An unknown and potentially large fraction of individual ichnofossils may be misidentified inorganic features or body fossils. Woodmorappe quotes uniformitarian ichnologists conceding that biogenic/abiogenic distinction "commonly relies on detailed analysis with an experienced eye" and that many outcrop arguments "go unresolved." He proposes a case-by-case "Certainty Index," which does not yet exist.
2. **Multiple origins, including for the two ichnogenera at the K-Pg horizon.** Some fossil *Zoophycos* may be body fossils of a sabellid worm rather than traces; *Chondrites* may have a plant origin, or be referable to burrows of the modern bivalve *Thyasira*. The trace-fossil/body-fossil dichotomy is argued not to be all-or-none even within a single ichnogenus. Since the extant biosphere is on this model an impoverished remnant of the antediluvian one, the known range of organism geometries is a poor guide to what could have produced a given structure.
3. **Construction rates are fast, and *Zoophycos* specifically is not a slow trace.** A large, diverse set of burrowers disturbs centimetres to tens of centimetres of sediment "in a matter of seconds to minutes"; entire beds can be overturned in less than a day; *Cruziana* and *Rusophycus* form within at most 4–5 days; *Rusophycus* is analogised to the modern crab *Emeritana*, which conceals itself in seconds. Woodmorappe states directly that "*Zoophycos* is no longer considered as a slowly-constructed ichnofossil."
4. **Multiple horizons can be bioturbated simultaneously.** The one-horizon-at-a-time assumption is what generates the time problem, and it is an assumption rather than an observation. Mechanisms offered: lenses of "fluffy" sediment, slurry-flow conditions with internal buoyancy, and the **PPLC hypothesis** (Penecontemporaneous Partially Lithified Crusts) — zones of rapid lithification alternating with Flood deposition, shielding buried organisms from overburden and permitting simultaneous bioturbation of a considerable thickness soon after deposition. The end result is described as "a thick but cryptically composite, highly bioturbed interval."
5. **Flood conditions are argued to favour rather than hinder bioturbation in one respect.** Under modern low depositional rates, seafloor sediments go anoxic at shallow depth; rapid Flood sedimentation repeatedly delivers pulses of oxygenated sediment, so oxygen is less likely to have been the limiting factor.

**Fit with this programme specifically.** This reading is consistent with, and sharpened by, the founding research's own insistence that the Flood year "cannot simply be unconstrained chaos" and supplies hundreds of days for evolving quasi-stable local environments ([`../../../research/GPT-hydrotectonic-flood-model.md`](../../../research/GPT-hydrotectonic-flood-model.md), ~line 535). The hydrotectonic model adds something Woodmorappe's general treatment does not have: a *mechanism* for the alternation his PPLC hypothesis requires. Hydroplaning under near-lithostatic pore pressure ([`../kinematics/README.md`](../kinematics/README.md)) is inherently episodic at the local scale — pore-pressure surges, transport pulses, and settling intervals — which is the depositional rhythm PPLC needs. Whether that connection can be made quantitative is open work, and would be a genuine contribution back rather than a borrowing.

**What Woodmorappe himself concedes** (rule 3 — reported, not smoothed): "Very thick sedimentary lithologies that have been extensively bioturbated present more of a challenge to a Flood model," and thick sequences at high ichnofabric index in undoubted superposition "may have had insufficient time." He works the hard case explicitly rather than avoiding it — 48 Cambrian sections, 1,151 m of strata in the western USA, the majority of that thickness at ichnofabric index ≥3. He states that knowledge of organisms "transported en masse and then suddenly encased by thick sediments" is "currently very sparse," and calls for an experiment, not yet performed, to determine how long suddenly-buried organisms continue burrowing in uncompressed sediment pockets.

**The surviving open question, in his framing rather than an imported one:** not whether any individual trace can form quickly — that is answered — but whether thick, superposed, high-ichnofabric-index intervals can form in the time available.

**Discriminator candidate generated here, deferred.** PPLC predicts penecontemporaneous lithification fronts interleaved with bioturbated horizons; ordinary historical geology has no reason to expect them.
