# Rheology / Heat Budget

Belt content — and the programme's critical-path gate. See [`../../3.3-prediction/discriminators.md`](../../3.3-prediction/discriminators.md).

**From the initial research:** required transient viscosity during the catastrophic interval is 10¹³–10¹⁴ Pa·s (vs. present upper-mantle viscosity 10²⁰–10²¹ Pa·s), derived by requiring deformation stress stay near rock-strength limits (~10⁸ Pa) at the kinematically-required velocities. A qualitative feedback mechanism is proposed (hydration → weakening → velocity increase → deformation heating → further weakening), consistent with Baumgardner-style runaway-subduction proposals in the creationist literature and with mainstream findings that olivine rheology is strongly water- and temperature-dependent. The magnitude of the required drop (6–8 orders of magnitude from present values) is stated; whether the feedback can plausibly reach it is not shown.

**Independently checked (2026-08-16):** the model's own mechanism is water content in transition-zone minerals (ringwoodite/wadsleyite), not partial melt — so the requirement should be checked against water-specific weakening, not a partial-melt floor. It was initially benchmarked against the wrong regime (partial-melt subduction-channel weak zones, ~10¹⁹ Pa·s), giving a 5–6 order-of-magnitude gap. Checked against the correct mechanism instead: Fei et al. 2017 (*Science Advances*, "A nearly water-saturated mantle transition zone inferred from mineral viscosity") measured dislocation mobility (inversely related to viscosity) in ringwoodite specifically, finding ~3.5 orders of magnitude reduction from anhydrous to fully water-saturated — the largest documented water-specific effect on the correct mineral. Starting from a standard transition-zone baseline (~10²¹ Pa·s) and stacking that effect reaches ~10¹⁷–10¹⁸ Pa·s, still short of the required 10¹³–10¹⁴ Pa·s by **~3–4 orders of magnitude** — a real, correctly-targeted mechanism that narrows but does not close the gap. See [`../../../dev-notes/20260816-water-weakening-mechanism.md`](../../../dev-notes/20260816-water-weakening-mechanism.md) for sourcing — confidence upgraded to HIGH (2026-08-16) after obtaining Fei et al.'s primary full text via PMC5462500; the dev-note also carries the authors' own stated caveats (postglacial-rebound-region inference, temperature dependence, unresolved conductivity discrepancies).

## Rescoped, 2026-08-16 — localized to subduction channels, not the bulk transition zone

This gate no longer gates *everything* downstream. JD's prior hydroplaning slice ([`../kinematics/README.md`](../kinematics/README.md)) supplies a separate, quantified mechanism (Terzaghi effective-stress collapse, ~100× heat reduction) for bulk lateral translation that does not depend on mantle viscosity at all. This gate is now scoped narrowly to the vertical question only: can slabs descend into the transition zone within the Flood year, in the smaller volume actually occupied by active subduction channels — not the whole transition zone.

That rescoping is a genuine narrowing, not a rhetorical one: it's the same move mainstream geodynamics already makes with "subduction-channel weak zones" (the very comparison this gate was originally, wrongly, benchmarked against — see the entry above). A localized volume needing 3–4 orders of magnitude of softening, where water content is plausibly most concentrated anyway (the down-going slab), is a materially easier case than the same requirement applied globally. It is **not yet a closed case** — no calculation has been done showing the localized-channel version is achievable, only that the ask is smaller than previously framed.

**Open, added to ROADMAP item 1:** a vertical-descent rate calculation for subduction-channel material specifically has not been done — the existing kinematics work (`../kinematics/README.md`) covers lateral hydroplaning velocity, not vertical slab descent. This is a distinct calculation from both the lateral-velocity reconciliation and the viscosity-magnitude question, and none of the three has been completed yet.

**Open (ROADMAP item 1, gating the vertical-subduction / deep-water-transport half of the model):** quantify total tectonic heat generated at the required velocities against total plausible heat-removal capacity (evaporation, convection, precipitation, radiation, hypercane-scale circulation), following the initial research's own proposed test — "give the hypercane hypothesis its best possible quantitative case." This is the programme's first computational work; scripts land in [`code/`](code/) under the discipline in [`../../00-program-methods/research-practices.md`](../../../04-construct/methods.md) §Computational work — sourced parameters, deterministic and re-runnable from the script's own header, no undisclosed fitting, refuted attempts kept and labeled rather than deleted.

## Energy-partition completeness check, 2026-08-17 — the programme's first computational work

Code: [`code/energy_partition_bounds.py`](code/energy_partition_bounds.py). Deterministic, stdlib-only, no fitted parameters. Run with `python3 02-theory/rheology/code/energy_partition_bounds.py`. Falsification conditions F1–F3 were fixed in the script header before the run, per [`research-practices.md`](../../../04-construct/methods.md) rule 1.

**What prompted it.** An external objection (2026-08-17): *"You need energy to start the move, and an equal and opposite amount of energy to stop it. Friction is the loss in the middle of this. Reduce friction and you still have to deal with the car crash."* The objection located a real gap in the documentation rather than in the physics: the published partition in [`POSITION-PAPER.md`](../../../paper/POSITION-PAPER.md) §3.2 (94.2% residual PE / 5.0% friction / 0.5% viscous / 0.25% seismic) partitions the gravitational potential energy release only, and the repository contained no kinetic-energy accounting anywhere. The impact-trigger energy of Figure 1 was likewise unquantified.

**F1 — bulk kinetic energy (the "stopping energy"). PASS.** At this programme's own stated velocity (9–40 cm/s), bulk KE of the moving continental mass is 0.011% to 0.416% of the friction allocation, against a declared threshold of 1%. Omitting the term from §3.2 is harmless. Deposited entirely in the present ocean it would produce ~10⁻⁵ to ~4×10⁻⁴ K. The objection does not land: the stopping term sits roughly three orders of magnitude below the friction term it was claimed to dominate, because kinetic energy scales as $v^2$ and this mechanism is slow-over-vast-distance, the regime where path-integrated dissipation dominates endpoint kinetic energy.

**F2 — velocity ceiling. New internal constraint.** Requiring bulk KE not to exceed the budget yields a hard ceiling of **27.7 to 38.6 m/s** (range reflecting the continental-mass dispute below), and a far tighter practical ceiling of **6.2 to 8.6 m/s** if KE is held within the friction allocation. This independently corroborates the rejection of the source document's disputed 30 m/s figure ([`../kinematics/README.md`](../kinematics/README.md), §velocity reconciliation): at 30 m/s bulk KE is 60% to 117% of the *entire* 10²⁵ J budget, leaving nothing for the 94.2% residual-PE term the partition requires. The 30 m/s figure was already rejected on a unit-conversion argument; it is now also refuted on energy conservation, by a separate route. **Forbids:** any future kinematic revision proposing continental velocity above this ceiling.

**F3 — survivable bulk-heating bound. PASS, with large margin.** Per JD (2026-08-17), **Chicxulub is a member of this model's initiating swarm**, not an external yardstick. That makes it an observational anchor rather than a hypothetical: whatever a swarm member does thermally, there is a worked example of it in the actual record.

Tested against marine survivability rather than against the tectonic partition, the numbers are not close. Ocean uptake reaching the upper thermal limit of marine life (+20 to +24 K above present) is 1.18×10²⁶ to 1.41×10²⁶ J, which is **11.8× the entire 10²⁵ J gravitational budget**. Deposit the whole budget in the ocean and it warms 1.7 K; the model's actual dissipated share warms it 0.098 K. A single Chicxulub adds 0.071 K, 0.22 K, or 9.86 K across the cited estimate range. Swarm sizes affordable within the survivability bound on bulk heating alone: **281 bodies** at the commonly cited energy, **90** at the assessment low, **2** at the assessment high. Bulk ocean heating is simply not the binding constraint on this mechanism, which means the whole objection class that targets it fails generally rather than in the instance.

**F3_CONTROL — the refuted first framing, retained in the code per [`research-practices.md`](../../../04-construct/methods.md) rules 6 and 11.** The first version of F3, written and superseded the same day, asked whether impact energy fits inside the model's own 5.75% dissipation allocation and reported a tension (0.01 to 1.38 Chicxulub-equivalents affordable). It died on two counts, both worth keeping visible. **(1) Category error:** the 5.75% figure partitions the GPE *release*; it is not a global thermal budget, and impacts are a separate energy input rather than a competing claim on the tectonic partition. **(2) Empirically falsified premise:** it assumed full thermalization into the ocean, and the one swarm member in the observational record shows the opposite. Chicxulub's dominant global thermal signature was net *cooling* — dust and sulfate aerosol loading drove surface air temperature down by as much as ~26 K with sub-freezing conditions persisting for years — followed by CO₂ greenhouse warming of only 1 to 5 K. The objection's implicit "impact energy becomes ocean heat" model is contradicted by the best-observed impact in the record.

**F4 — what actually binds the swarm. Open, named rather than computed.** Since bulk thermal energy does not constrain swarm size, the real limits are radiative and climatic: aerosol and dust loading and the resulting shading excursion (which runs *opposite* in sign to the objection's assumption), CO₂ and sulfate injection from carbonate and evaporite target rock, ejecta re-entry radiation, and the survivability of the biota the narrative requires to come through. Stating the rival at its strongest ([rule 4](../../../04-construct/methods.md)): the mainstream account has *one* such impact driving a mass extinction, so a swarm is by construction a larger perturbation, and that is the genuine cost this model has to price — in climate response, not in joules. Resolving it requires a modeled aerosol/radiative response, not attempted here.

One further note carried from the founding research: it already argues impacts should not be "the engines that literally shove the continents across the planet," with negative slab buoyancy and gravitational disequilibrium supplying "much of the sustained mechanical energy," which it says "greatly reduces the impact-energy requirement" ([`../../../research/GPT-hydrotectonic-flood-model.md`](../../../research/GPT-hydrotectonic-flood-model.md) lines 199–217). That bounds what the trigger must *supply*, not what a body of given size *deposits*, so it bears on the mechanism rather than on F3 or F4.

**F4 update, 2026-08-19 — the first constraint on swarm *timing* rather than swarm *size*.** F4 named the binding limits as radiative and climatic and left them uncomputed. A separate route now supplies one that is neither: the impact-lofted precipitation mechanism ([`../water-ledger/README.md`](../water-ledger/README.md) §Precipitation mechanism, JD-directed) requires the swarm be **distributed across the 40 days**, because ballistically lofted water returns in 1.5–5 minutes and an instantaneous swarm therefore yields one deluge rather than sustained rain. This does not bound swarm *size*, which F4 still leaves open — it bounds swarm *schedule*, which nothing previously did. It is consistent with the simultaneous-swarm radiometric assumption in [`../stratigraphy/README.md`](../stratigraphy/README.md), since 40 days is ~10⁻⁷ Myr.

**Downstream, unresolved and flagged rather than absorbed:** if Chicxulub is a swarm member, the K-Pg boundary falls *inside* the catastrophic interval. [`../stratigraphy/README.md`](../stratigraphy/README.md) and [`POSITION-PAPER.md`](../../../paper/POSITION-PAPER.md) §6.2 currently record K-Pg as one of three tested-and-rejected candidate boundaries with no positive candidate standing. These two positions need reconciling. The founding research anticipated the tension from the other side, noting K-Pg is "the strongest candidate if we weight your cosmological-impact forcing heavily" and is the one boundary with "an actual crater and ejecta system." Not resolved here — this is a belt commitment awaiting JD's explicit call, not an inference to be written in silently.

**Disputed inputs, reported rather than smoothed** ([`research-practices.md`](../../../04-construct/methods.md) rule 3). Continental crust mass is carried as a range, 1.34×10²² to 2.60×10²² kg, because published estimates disagree by roughly a factor of two depending on whether submerged margins are included and which density model is used (2600 to 2835 kg/m³); the total-crust figure including oceanic crust is ~2.77×10²² kg. Sources: CRUST2.0-based mass/composition estimates (Peters & Sobolev, AGU 2007 V33A-1161) resting on Christensen & Mooney (1995), *JGR Solid Earth* 100, 9761–9788. Chicxulub impactor energy is likewise carried as a range, 1.3×10²⁴ to 5.8×10²⁵ J from the multi-model assessment in Durand-Manterola & Cordero-Tercero (2014), arXiv:1403.6391, against commonly cited single figures near 10²³ and 4.18×10²³ J. Marine upper thermal limits are from Nguyen et al. (2011), *PLoS ONE* 6(12): e29340 — 37–41 °C for subtidal ectotherms under rapid heating, falling to ~35.4–40 °C under chronic heating, which against a present ~16 °C mean sea-surface temperature gives the +20 to +24 K headroom used in F3. All at confidence **MEDIUM** (specific figures from secondary summaries; none of the three primaries read in full).

**What this does not resolve.** Heat *removal* capacity is untouched, so ROADMAP item 1 proper remains open, and this script bounds generation terms only. The viscosity gate above is unaffected. The 10²⁵ J budget and its four-way split are imported from Longmire (2025) v2.5 and taken as given here: this checks the partition for **completeness, not correctness**, and every ratio above moves if the underlying budget does. Vertical/subduction energetics are not addressed. F3's survivability proxy is bulk-ocean only and says nothing about atmospheric, radiative, or aerosol effects, which for impacts are the dominant terms — that is exactly what F4 names and leaves open.

## Heat-removal capacity, 2026-08-18 — ROADMAP item 1 proper, `WP-HTF-0006`

Code: [`code/heat_removal_capacity.py`](code/heat_removal_capacity.py). Deterministic, stdlib-only, no fitted parameters. Falsification conditions F7–F9 fixed in the script header before the run, per [`research-practices.md`](../../../04-construct/methods.md) rule 1. **Covers the LATERAL translation heat term only** — the vertical/subduction-channel term (F9) is not addressed; see below.

**What prompted it.** ROADMAP item 1, the programme's own declared gate on everything, had never been quantified on the removal side — `energy_partition_bounds.py` (2026-08-17) bounded *generation* terms only. POSITION-PAPER §2 states the actual constraint this mechanism answers: naive full-friction sliding at continental velocities implies a sustained local flux "on the order of 600 W/m², roughly half the power of direct solar irradiance" — and §3.1 claims Terzaghi collapse means "the heat was never generated," a claim held qualitatively since stand-up and never checked against a number.

**F7 — local sustained flux. PASS, 2.1× margin.** This programme's own already-published dissipated-energy share (5.75% of the 1×10²⁵ J budget, `energy_partition_bounds.py`), spread over the actively-translating crust area and the Flood-year duration, gives a sustained local flux of **64–135 W/m² — only 11–23% of the naive 600 W/m² figure** (a 4.4–9.4× reduction). This is the first quantitative confirmation of §3.1's claim rather than an assertion of it. Checked against the runaway-greenhouse radiative ceiling (280–310 W/m², Simpson-Nakajima limit — Ingersoll 1969; Nakajima, Hayashi & Abe 1992, *J. Atmos. Sci.* 49; reviewed in Goldblatt & Watson 2012, *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. A* 370; confidence MEDIUM, general literature knowledge, not independently retrieved primary text this session), the worst case sits comfortably below it. **Hypercane-scale heat export is not necessary for the lateral term alone** — a genuine, informative negative-in-a-good-way result relative to the founding research's own instruction to give that hypothesis its strongest case: ordinary radiative capacity already suffices once the Terzaghi-collapse reduction (not the naive full-friction figure) is used correctly.

**F8 — global-mean stacking check. PASS, thin margin — read under rule 21.** The more physically correct check: this programme's dissipated flux, diluted over the *whole* planet (35.7 W/m²), added to Earth's present-day mean outgoing radiation baseline (240 W/m², standard climatology figure), gives a **total required flux of 275.7 W/m² against a 280–310 W/m² ceiling** — a pass, but using roughly 98.5% of the available headroom at the low end of a literature range that is itself disputed (some spectroscopy-refined treatments give ~282 W/m², not 310). On citation precision alone this result sits close enough to the edge that a different, equally defensible source figure could flip it. **Surfaced before being written down** (rule 11) with two live readings — a real, demanding margin a designed singular event happens to clear, or a result too close to my own sourcing uncertainty to call resolved. **JD's ruling (2026-08-18): PASS-with-thin-margin under [`research-practices.md`](../../../04-construct/methods.md) rule 21** — the same interpretive posture as WP-HTF-0003's pore-pressure finding: an honestly-derived value sitting close to a hard physical ceiling is read as a genuine feature of the mechanism, not a defect, given the hard core's own design commitment. The thin margin is recorded plainly rather than rounded away.

**F9 — vertical/subduction-channel heat. Not computed, named per rule 18.** No channel geometry (width, length, depth extent) exists anywhere in this belt to derive an energy or power figure from — only the required *viscosity* range above (10¹³–10¹⁴ Pa·s), not Joules or Watts. **F7/F8 do not close ROADMAP item 1 for the vertical half of the mechanism.** Establishing subduction-channel dimensions from subduction-zone geology is required first and remains open, distinct from the vertical-descent rate calculation already flagged open above.

**What this resolves and what it doesn't.** The lateral heat generation-vs-removal question — half of ROADMAP item 1 — is now answered favorably, with F7 comfortable and F8 thin-but-passing. The rheology/viscosity gate above (vertical subduction) is **unaffected** — this is a different energy budget entirely (atmosphere-ocean radiative capacity, not mantle viscosity) and remains 3–4 orders of magnitude short on its own terms.

## Read against Baumgardner's CPT, 2026-08-19 — a possible category error in this gate

Full research pass in [`../../../dev-notes/20260819-baumgardner-cpt-comparison.md`](../../../dev-notes/20260819-baumgardner-cpt-comparison.md); both primary ICC papers retrieved and read, confidence **HIGH**. This programme has cited Baumgardner-style runaway subduction by name since stand-up (paragraph 1 above) without ever mining it for its actual constitutive law. Doing so surfaces three things.

**1. His published runaway range reaches this programme's requirement.** Baumgardner (2003) reports simulated runaway viscosities of **10¹³–10¹⁸ Pa·s**, against a pre-runaway mid-mantle value of 3×10²⁴ Pa·s — a ~10⁹-fold drop, carried to completion in 2D for the first time using Yang's matrix-dependent-transfer multigrid solver, and with plastic yield included. The bottom of that range *is* this gate's required 10¹³–10¹⁴ Pa·s.

**2. The comparison class this gate uses may be the wrong one.** Fei et al. (2017) is a **static** law — water content in ringwoodite at fixed state, ~3.5 orders. Baumgardner's weakening is an **emergent output of a dynamical instability**: Arrhenius thermal weakening ($\eta \propto e^{E^*/RT}$) combined with two laboratory-measured deformation-rate weakening regimes on olivine — dislocation creep (strength $\propto \dot\gamma^{-2/3}$) and plastic yield (strength $\propto \dot\gamma^{-1}$) — where heating feeds weakening feeds faster deformation feeds more heating. A runaway's terminal viscosity cannot be obtained by looking up a static coefficient, and this gate has only ever been benchmarked against static coefficients. **This does not close the gate.** It identifies that "3–4 orders short" may be measuring the wrong quantity, which is a distinct and much cheaper thing to check than closing the gap itself.

**3. The budget comparison — quantified, and corrected 2026-08-19 as to what it implies.** Independently computed (whole-mantle recycling of ocean lithosphere: 3.1×10¹⁴ m² × 100 km × 3300 kg/m³, ~2% density excess, 2890 km descent): **~5.8×10²⁸ J**, against 3.5×10²⁷ J to boil the present ocean — so **5–17× ocean-vaporisation energy**. This programme's entire budget is 10²⁵ J. **CPT's heat problem is roughly 5,800× this programme's**, because CPT moves the whole mantle and this mechanism moves crustal blocks on a 15 km detachment (`WP-HTF-0003`).

**What this does *not* imply, corrected on JD's framing (2026-08-19).** The first version of this section said adopting Baumgardner's route "would reimport a budget three orders of magnitude larger." That is package-shaped reasoning and is wrong as a general statement: **the budget travels with whole-mantle overturn, not with the components.** The two harvest targets identified here — the **Gruntfest (1963) shear-layer runaway criterion** and **Kirby's (1983) olivine deformation map** — are both *upstream* of Baumgardner's architecture. Gruntfest is a general stability result for any shear zone; Kirby is laboratory mineral physics that Baumgardner applied rather than produced. Neither carries whole-mantle overturn with it, and taking them costs nothing architecturally. What *is* inseparable from the 5.8×10²⁸ J is the whole-mantle architecture itself, along with the steam-jet cooling (which fails an enthalpy ceiling, see the dev-note), the deep cold-slab prediction (fails discriminator condition 2), and the accelerated-decay dependency (Baumgardner cites Humphreys/RATE; this programme's DFM $R_0$ route avoids it). Those four stay behind. The correction is recorded rather than silently rewritten, per rule 6.

**Still true, and it is a scoping fact rather than a cost:** his weakening operates at ~0.8 $T_m$ — mantle temperatures — so it has no purchase on the cold 15 km crustal detachment where Terzaghi collapse is the right mechanism. **The two mechanisms are complementary by depth, not competing.** The harvest is therefore split accordingly: Gruntfest to the lateral detachment (`WP-HTF-0012`, reachable now), the constitutive law to the vertical channel (gated on channel geometry, the same quantity F9 names).

**This also constrains what this programme should claim against CPT.** Baumgardner's own heat defence — *"no evidence of extreme temperatures associated with the runaway process... because the rate of deformational heating is proportional to the viscosity, which is diminished on the order of a billion-fold"* — is a **local rate** argument at fixed strain rate. It does not touch the integrated budget, which is set by gravitational potential energy release and is independent of rheology. F8 above is precisely the global-mean check CPT's papers do not report, which is a genuine methodological advantage worth stating plainly and worth *not* overstating: F8 passed thin, under rule 21.

**One critique of CPT this programme should not repeat** (rule 4 cuts both ways). The Matsumura/NCSE objection that lowering viscosity "removes the heat source the model needs to accelerate the subduction" is confused: dissipation at fixed stress is $\tau^2/\eta$, which *rises* as viscosity falls, and the driver is buoyancy, not frictional heat. The companion claim that thermal diffusivity would need to increase 10,000-fold could not be traced to a primary calculation — secondary attribution only, **UNCERTAIN**. Noted here so this programme does not inherit a bad objection while making a good one.
