# Initial conditions — lower supercontinental elevation, rich biodiversity

**Status: open.** Claims stated, not yet checked against literature or reconciled with existing belt content. Not yet promoted anywhere.

## The claim

JD, 2026-08-17: capture two additions to the pre-Flood initial condition ($S_0$, per `02-theory/kinematics/README.md`'s Araratia reconstruction) —

1. **Lower elevation for the supercontinent.** Present-day relief, including Everest's 8.8 km summit, is not read as pre-Flood terrain the flood water had to cover. It's read as substantially a product of the same catastrophic continental-collision event this programme's mechanism already describes (`02-theory/kinematics/README.md`, `02-theory/rheology/README.md`), the same way ordinary geology credits the India-Asia collision with building the Himalayas, just compressed into the Flood year. Pre-Flood Araratia is correspondingly lower-relief.
2. **Rich biodiversity as an initial condition.** Pre-Flood biological diversity is deployed rich from the start, not accumulated gradually. Consistent with hard-core commitment 4 (functionally mature deployment at creation, importing DFM) already applied to the crust in `01-hypothesis/hard-core.md` — this extends the same "mature at deployment, not accumulated" reading from geology to biology.

## Why this needed capturing before treating it as settled

Both points surfaced today as inferences drawn *from* the model rather than statements *in* it, and the programme's own discipline (`00-program-methods/research-practices.md`) is not to let an inference quietly ride along as though it were already a checked position.

**Topography.** Came up defending the model against an external heat-budget critique that assumed present Everest elevation as the water depth requirement (see `research/GPT-hydrotectonic-flood-model.md`-adjacent correspondence, 2026-08-17 rebuttal exchange). The kinematics doc already has continental blocks traveling thousands of km (2,000–6,000 avg, 8,000–12,000+ max) within the Flood year — collision at that displacement is orogenic on any account, mainstream or catastrophist. But `02-theory/kinematics/README.md` states $S_0$ only as "a Pangaea-type" reconstruction target with no elevation profile, and the position paper (`00-program-methods/POSITION-PAPER.md`) doesn't state a paleo-relief claim anywhere. The inference is very likely correct and consistent with the model's own mechanics, but it was not yet a stated position when it was used defensively, which is a gap worth closing rather than a fact this note is re-deriving.

**Biodiversity.** Not yet connected to any belt document. It bears directly on discriminator 1 in `03-prediction/discriminators.md` ("ecological-province co-occurrence": taxa co-occurring geographically before the catastrophe should co-occur stratigraphically more often than taxa separated by habitat geography) — that discriminator implicitly assumes a rich, differentiated pre-Flood biosphere with real ecological-province structure, which is exactly what "rich biodiversity as an initial condition" would supply, but the connection has not been drawn in the belt.

## What's not yet done

- No elevation profile or paleo-relief figure has been specified for $S_0$. "Lower than present" is qualitative; whether this needs to be quantified (and to what precision) before it can do the rebuttal work asked of it today is undetermined.
- No literature check has been run on either claim — not on pre-Flood/pre-catastrophe paleotopography reconstruction in the flood-geology literature the programme already draws on (Oard, ICR, CRS), and not on any biodiversity-at-deployment claim in the DFM companion framework or elsewhere.
- The relationship between "rich initial biodiversity" and the existing stratigraphic mapping (`02-theory/stratigraphy/README.md`, itself under active revision per the appraisal log) hasn't been worked out — a richer pre-Flood biosphere changes what the ecological-province discriminator predicts and how it's evaluated.
