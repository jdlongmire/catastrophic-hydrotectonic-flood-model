## The single worst instance: `methodology.md` line 41

> *"the transient mantle viscosity required for continental translation at the required Flood-year rate (~10¹³–10¹⁴ Pa·s) is roughly **five to six orders of magnitude** below any viscosity invoked anywhere in mainstream geodynamics, including the most extreme **partial-melt weak-zone estimates (~10¹⁹ Pa·s floor)**."*

Both halves were corrected **on 2026-08-16**, three days before this re-grade, and the correction is in the appraisal log:

- The partial-melt benchmark is **the wrong mechanism.** This programme's mechanism is water content in ringwoodite/wadsleyite, not partial melt.
- Benchmarked correctly against **Fei et al. 2017** (*Science Advances*, primary full text via PMC5462500, confidence **HIGH**) — ~3.5 orders of water-specific viscosity reduction on the correct mineral — the residual gap is **~3–4 orders, not 5–6.**

The appraisal's own entry for that date says it plainly: *"the gate's stated difficulty was wrong: benchmarked against an unrelated mechanism, **overstating the gap by ~2 orders of magnitude**. Corrected forward in `rheology/README.md` and `discriminators.md` rather than left standing."*

It was corrected in those two files. **`methodology.md` was missed**, and has been overstating this programme's own gating problem by two orders of magnitude, in present tense, in a Tier 0 file, ever since.

The same paragraph's *"Nothing else in the belt matters until this is addressed"* is also now partly discharged: `WP-HTF-0006` closed the lateral half (F7 PASS 2.1×, F8 PASS thin under rule 21).

**Why item 5 is a genre error, stated so it is not mistaken for special pleading.** The objection was *"read as a sequence with zero forbidding content added at any step."* That is a hypothetico-deductive scorecard — it grades on pre-registered forbidding content. Rule 24(d) demoted pre-registration from gate to discipline, on Cleland's own ground that the paradigm smoking gun (K-Pg iridium) was not predicted in advance. Under the forensic standard, testing four candidate horizons against evidence and eliminating them is **eliminative inference**, which rule 24(b) makes a first-class result.

**What survives, and it is not nothing.** Rule 24(a) is explicit that *"the re-base adds this requirement while removing pre-registration as a gate; net, the bar went up, not down."* The honest forensic restatement of the original concern is therefore **sharper**, not softer: the three revisions narrowed the live hypothesis set without ever naming **what trace would pick this programme out from its competitors** — which is Cleland's actual failure state, the just-so story. That charge stands, and it is the one the appraisal should carry.

**And rule 4a on item 5.** The rival is not handed this card either. Deep-time geology has revised boundary definitions, GSSP placements and stage boundaries repeatedly under evidence, and is not scored as degenerating for it. Applying "revised under pressure without adding forbidding content" to one account and not the other is the asymmetry rule 4a exists to stop.

## The same error, committed fresh, in this session

The PI-01 close-out written earlier today scored the increment at **"committed value delivered: 14 of 34"** and led with the count.

That is the identical failure shape rule 4a corrected, applied to a different object:

- **It graded plan-conformance when PI-01's own stated value criterion is epistemic advance** — *"Value is epistemic: does it advance the programme toward being able to be wrong?"* By that criterion the unplanned work was worth **more** than what it displaced: registering E1 and E2 moved the programme from zero forbidding claims to two, which is the largest structural gain since stand-up, and it was not on the plan at all.
- **It contradicted the increment's own cadence rule** — *"an increment closes on resolution, not on the clock. Research does not commit to a discovery, only to running the attempt."* A day-one plan for an exploratory programme being overtaken by discovery is that rule working, not a delivery miss.
- **It handed the card to nobody else.** No comparable research increment was scored the same way, which is precisely the rule 4a objection.

**What survives from that close-out, on its own grounds:** the O4 deferral. That cost does *not* rest on a scorecard comparison — `appraisal.md` names the chronology partition **by name** as one of this programme's four self-imposed degeneration markers. Rule 4a's recalibration was explicit that *"the programme keeps its high internal standard in full."* An internal marker the programme set for itself is not an asymmetry with the rival; it is the standard the programme chose. It stays.

So the close-out is corrected to: **one specific deferral recorded as a cost, no headline count** — rather than a 60%-undelivered framing that graded the wrong thing.
