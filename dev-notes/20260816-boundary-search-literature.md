# Boundary-search literature — the "none survived" verdict was premature

**Status: resolved.** Promoted to `02-theory/stratigraphy/README.md`, `03-prediction/appraisal.md`, and the intro video narration.

## The claim

JD, reviewing the intro video's line "none of them survived contact with the evidence": pushed back on treating the three rejected boundaries (Cambrian, then P-T/T-J/K-Pg) as unequivocal failures without exploring the nuances first.

## What was checked

The "boundaries fail" verdict rests on one claim from the initial research transcript: paleosols, coal, and reefs recur past every candidate boundary. That claim was never checked against the actual flood-geology literature before landing in `appraisal.md` as a settled "degenerating" verdict. Pulled two primary sources:

- **Oard & Klevberg 2022, "Petrified Ideas of the Williston Basin — Part II: Fossil Wood," *CRSQ* 58:204–219.** Directly on point: field work at the Fort Union Group, Theodore Roosevelt National Park — the formation immediately above K-Pg, the exact interval the transcript cites against that boundary. Their field examination found no soil profile under the petrified stumps mainstream geologists (Biek & Gonzalez 2001) interpret as two successive paleosol-based forests — "distinct iron horizons and concretions associated with bentonite beds," not soil. Their read: transported log mats buried rapidly, not centuries of in-place forest growth.
- **Doyle 2017, "Do 'laterite' soils take a million years to form?" *Journal of Creation* 31(3):5–7.** Narrower scope (a specific soil type), and appropriately modest — its own conclusion is "more work clearly needs to be done," not a solved problem.

**Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH** for Oard & Klevberg — read in full primary text (not a search summary), direct field claims with photographs and citations. MEDIUM for Doyle — also full primary text, but the mechanism it leans on (transport, not faster in-situ weathering) is a different question than the one at hand.

## Disposition

The evidence cited against the three boundaries is real, and this doesn't reverse it — but whether that evidence is correctly identified as what it's claimed to be is a genuine, live dispute in the literature the initial research never checked. "None of them survived" overstated how settled that was. The accurate state: still an open question, not a closed one.
