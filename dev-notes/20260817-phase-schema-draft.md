## Why this is more than terminology

The repository currently says "Cambrian," "Permian-Triassic," "K-Pg" while denying those labels carry the time-assignments the standard model gives them. That borrows the rival's chronological framework and rejects its content in the same breath. A phase vocabulary anchored to *this model's own processes* removes the borrowing.

**And it may dissolve rather than solve the §6.2 problem.** The three failed boundary searches asked: *which single global horizon marks the Flood?* But the founding research already proposed a **diachronous propagation front** $t_C(x)$, and a diachronous front is a phase concept, not a horizon concept. Those searches may have failed because they sought the wrong kind of object. See §The boundary question, below — this turns out to align with an existing position in the literature.

## Prior art, researched (rule 8)

### Walker's biblical geologic model

Tas Walker, "A Biblical Geologic Model" (ICC proceedings), developed at biblicalgeology.net and applied to real field cases — the Great Artesian Basin, the Brisbane basement rocks, Banks Peninsula NZ. Confidence **MEDIUM** (assembled from multiple secondary summaries plus Walker's own chart titles; the ICC paper itself returned HTTP 500 and was not retrieved).

- **Creation event** — *Foundational* stage (2 days) + *Formative* stage (4 days).
- **Flood event, Inundatory stage** (~60 days), three phases: **Eruptive** (bursting of the springs of the deep, floodgates of heaven, rain begins), **Ascending** (rain continues, waters rise), **Zenithic** (waters at maximum, all earth covered).
- **Flood event, Recessive stage** (~300 days), two phases: **Abative** (sheet flow off a wholly submerged surface), **Dispersive** (flow divides into large channels as land emerges).
- Walker's chart headers also carry **Primordial**, **Residual**, and **Modern** periods bracketing the above.

**Walker's phases are defined by water level.** That is the axis he works on, and it is well chosen for field application.

### Oard's post-Flood ice age

Single ice age, under ~1,000 years (commonly cited ~700), driven by warm oceans pole-to-pole and top-to-bottom plus continents cooled in summer by stratospheric volcanic aerosols from post-Flood volcanism. CRSQ 1979; ICR 1990, *An Ice Age Caused by the Genesis Flood*. Multiple till sheets and moraine sequences — which mainstream geology reads as separate glaciations — are read as advance/retreat oscillations of the margins **within** the single event. Confidence **MEDIUM**.

**Singular adopted, on JD's question (2026-08-17), for a structural reason rather than a citational one:** the mechanism is a *relaxation from an initial condition*, not an oscillator. The ocean cools precisely because it powers the evaporation; the aerosols wash out as volcanism declines. There is no restoring force and no post-Flood mechanism to re-warm the ocean, so the driver cannot repeat. Milankovitch cycling is separately unavailable — precession ~23 kyr, obliquity ~41 kyr, eccentricity ~100 kyr, none of which fit inside 6,000 years. Adopting cyclicity would require inventing a new driver, i.e. adding a free parameter on the chronology axis, which is the one axis this programme's own appraisal already flags as accumulating interpretive freedom.

## The proposed schema — two axes, not one

**This is the contribution.** Walker's phases track *water level*. This programme has a mechanism Walker's general model does not, so it can track a second, orthogonal axis: **hydrotectonic state**. A rock unit is assigned on both. The axes are correlated but not identical, and separating them is what makes an assignment falsifiable rather than definitional.

## The boundary question — and a significant finding

**The Flood/post-Flood transition is not a horizon in this schema. It is a phase transition, and it is diachronous:** $t_C(x)$, varying by location.

That is not an evasion invented here. It corresponds to an **existing, third position in a live creationist debate**, which has three camps:

1. A **low** boundary, Paleozoic or below.
2. **K-Pg** — Snelling generally advocates this.
3. **Late Cenozoic** — Oard, on fourteen mostly-geological criteria including the Messinian salinity-crisis evaporites, the Absaroka Volcanics and Yellowstone fossil "forests," Miocene coal, the Hanna and South Caspian Basins, the African planation surface, and sheet gravel transport off south-central Asian mountains. Oard argues K-Pg advocates "have extreme difficulty explaining the fossils and sediments of the Cenozoic."
4. A **variable boundary** — evaluated per geographic area on its merits. Notably, **Snelling himself** placed the boundary in Israel at or near the top of the Oligocene despite generally advocating K-Pg.

Confidence **MEDIUM** (secondary summaries; primaries not retrieved).

**Three things follow, and JD should see all three.**

**First, the founding research independently arrived at the variable-boundary position.** $t_C(x)$ *is* the variable boundary. The programme's own proposal, reached without reference to this debate, matches an existing camp in it. That is corroboration of the framing, not novelty.

**Second, it complicates the K-Pg convergence.** The three converging lines flagged earlier pointed at K-Pg, which places this programme on Snelling's side against Oard — while the belt currently leans on Oard for the ice age and on Oard & Klevberg for the paleosol dispute. Using Oard's field results while rejecting his boundary is coherent, but it should be *deliberate* rather than accidental.

**Third, under a phase framework the paleocurrent evidence fits better than under either fixed boundary.** Coherence is reported as strong in the Paleozoic, weaker in the Mesozoic, scattered in the Cenozoic. That is a **declining envelope**, not a step. A fixed boundary at any single horizon predicts a step; a diachronous phase transition predicts exactly the observed decline. This is a point in favour of the phase reframe over the boundary question in either form.

## The discipline that keeps this from being insulation

A phase vocabulary defined only by the model, and mapped to rock units only by the model, cannot be wrong. Three rules, proposed as belt-level discipline:

1. **Every phase carries stated physical criteria** (the third column above). An assignment is a claim about signature, not a placement in a narrative.
2. **Assignments are recorded with their evidence and are revisable individually.** Moving one unit between phases is a local correction; moving the schema is a belt revision and gets logged in the appraisal as such.
3. **Standard-column names remain usable as *locational* shorthand** ("the unit at the K-Pg horizon") but never as *chronological* claims. The distinction is stated once in the belt and then held.

## Discriminator that falls out of the singular ice age

**Registered nowhere yet; proposed here.** A relaxation-driven single ice age predicts glacial advances forming **one build-and-decay envelope with fluctuations of declining amplitude**, because the ocean cools and the aerosols clear monotonically. Milankovitch-driven cycling predicts **quasi-periodic repetition at comparable amplitude with no envelope**. The two accounts forbid different things, the sample exists (till sheets, moraine sequences, their relative extents), and the test is envelope-shape versus periodicity. This is better-formed than two of the three candidates currently flagged.
