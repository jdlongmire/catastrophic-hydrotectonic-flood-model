# "Araratia" retired — the pre-Flood supercontinent is Pangaea

**2026-08-19, JD-directed.** *"Retire Araratia."* Adopted 2026-08-16 from the visualization set as
this programme's standing term for $S_0$; retired today.

## Spelling: Pangaea, not Pangea

Wegener's 1920 German coinage was *Pangäa*, from Greek παν (*pan*, "all") + Γαῖα (*Gaia*, "Earth").
The standard English transliteration of the αι digraph is *ae*, giving **Pangaea** — the form the
geological literature has printed since, and the one still used by *Geology*, *GSA Bulletin*, and the
stratigraphic literature this programme cites. *Pangea* is the American-simplified variant, the same
reduction that gives *paleontology* for *palaeontology*. Not wrong, but newer and less formal.

Two practical reasons beyond convention: this repo already used **Pangaea 9 times against Pangea
once**, so the traditional form is also the lower-churn choice; and the position paper sits on Zenodo
alongside literature using *ae*, where matching costs nothing and reads as native to the field.

## This is not only a word swap — it changes what the programme claims

Worth stating plainly, because the substantive move is easy to miss behind the cosmetic one, and
because [`research-practices.md`](../04-construct/methods.md) rule 23b makes the distinction
load-bearing.

A **bespoke name** ("Araratia") asserted that this programme's pre-Flood supercontinent was *its own
reconstruction*, distinct from the one conventional geology reconstructs. Adopting **Pangaea**
asserts the opposite: that $S_0$ **is** the supercontinent mainstream reconstruction already names,
and that the two accounts differ over its *timing and breakup mechanism* rather than over its
existence or its geometry.

That is a real narrowing of the dispute, and it cuts both ways:

- **Cost.** The programme can no longer treat the supercontinent's configuration as free. Pangaea has
  a published geometry, a fit, and a literature; adopting the name adopts the target. ROADMAP item 2
  (replace the anchor-distance proxy with an actual Pangaea-fit Euler-pole reconstruction) becomes
  *more* binding, not less, because the reconstruction being fit is now the conventional one by name.
- **Benefit.** It removes a gratuitous divergence. A bespoke name for an object both accounts accept
  invites the reading that the programme is inventing objects, which is precisely the "just-so story"
  failure mode [`methodology.md`](../04-construct/methodology.md) §Genre names after Cleland. Sharing
  the rival's own vocabulary for a shared object concentrates the disagreement where it actually is.

## What was changed, and what deliberately was not

Fifteen occurrences across ten files, classified into three treatments rather than swept blindly —
the same classifier discipline that saved the 2026-08-18 repo rename from corrupting 35 of 46 matches.

**Renamed (living belt content, 5 occurrences, 4 files).** `hard-core.md` commitment 4;
`stratigraphy/README.md` phase table P0/P1; `kinematics/README.md` opening (rewritten, not
substituted, since the old text explained the Araratia adoption); `POSITION-PAPER.md` §1 commitment 4.

**Not renamed — dated records (5 occurrences, 3 files).** `appraisal.md` rows for 2026-08-16, and
both `20260817-*` dev-notes. These record what was decided *then*, and the decision then was
Araratia. Editing them would falsify the record; a new appraisal row records the retirement instead.
Same posture as rule 6 and as morals-layer practice on append-only history.

**Not renamed — descriptions of already-published artifacts (5 occurrences, 3 files).**
`assets/videos/intro/social-media-summaries.md` (×2), `assets/videos/global-intro/social-media-summaries.md`,
and `assets/images/README.md`. The shipped videos and rendered PNGs *say* "Araratia"; the markdown
describes them. Editing the description to disagree with the artifact makes the record less accurate,
not more. `assets/images/README.md` gains a stale-on-this-point note instead.
